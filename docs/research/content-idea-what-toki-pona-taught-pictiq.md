# Content idea: What Toki Pona Taught Pictiq About What Not to Say

Status: planning/research note; not a drafted article.

The Toki Pona interoperability study initially looked like a vocabulary-mapping exercise. Its more important result was showing that lexical absence is not necessarily a protocol deficiency.

The study helped reveal:

- Embodied versus Standalone Communication;
- `NONE` as several different states rather than one failure state;
- modifiers instead of lexical words;
- parametric color rather than a fixed color vocabulary;
- scoped entity symbols instead of spelling names;
- grammar intentionally remaining outside Pictiq;
- the principle that Pictiq should encode only what the communication surface cannot already provide.

Potential formats: Medium, Substack, or a future Pictiq / visual-languages book.
