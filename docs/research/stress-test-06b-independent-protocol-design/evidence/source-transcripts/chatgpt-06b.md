Хорошо. Начну именно с архитектуры, пока без попытки нарисовать полный словарь.

## **Часть 1\. Architecture**

Я бы исходил не из вопроса «как заменить слова картинками?», а из другого:

> Как передать минимальное количество структурированного смысла так, чтобы незнакомый человек мог разумно догадаться о нём, а машина могла разобрать его однозначно?

Это приводит к нескольким решениям.

### **1\. Не визуальный язык, а визуальный протокол**

Система не должна пытаться выразить всё, что умеет естественный язык.

Её рабочая область:

**объект \+ действие/состояние \+ свойства \+ количество \+ отношение \+ коммуникативное намерение.**

Например:

`WATER + COLD`

`3 × BOTTLE + WATER`

`TAXI + NEED`

`TOILET + WHERE?`

Это сознательно ближе к дорожным знакам, интерфейсам и информационным схемам, чем к письменному языку.

Фраза вроде «Мне кажется, что вчера он был немного расстроен» вообще не обязана иметь точное представление.

Это важное ограничение. Без него Core неизбежно разрастается.

---

## **2\. Meaning first, syntax second**

Каждое сообщение представляет маленькую семантическую конструкцию.

У неё есть **ядро**, а вокруг него модификаторы.

Условно:

`[INTENT] [QUANTITY] [ENTITY] [PROPERTY] [ACTION/RELATION] [TARGET]`

Но это не означает, что всегда нужны шесть позиций.

Например:

`WATER`

может означать просто концепт воды.

`WATER + COLD`

создаёт уточнённый концепт:

**cold water**

А:

`? + POSSESS/AVAILABLE + WATER`

создаёт:

**Is water available / Do you have water?**

Главное правило: символ должен сохранять приблизительно одинаковое значение независимо от конструкции.

---

## **3\. Три класса элементов**

Я бы жёстко разделил систему на три слоя.

### **A. Entities**

То, о чём мы говорим.

Например:

`WATER`  
`PERSON`  
`TOILET`  
`VEHICLE`  
`MONEY`  
`PLACE`

Обычно это наиболее иконические символы.

### **B. Operators**

Они изменяют отношения между сущностями.

Например:

`?` question

`×` quantity

`→` movement/direction

`¬` negation

`=` affirmation/match

`!` warning/attention

Операторов должно быть очень мало, потому что именно они фактически образуют грамматику.

### **C. Qualifiers**

Свойства объекта или действия:

`COLD`  
`HOT`  
`BIG`  
`SMALL`  
`GOOD`  
`BAD`

Но здесь я сразу ввёл бы сильное ограничение:

**qualifier не должен становиться отдельным символом, если значение можно достаточно понятно построить композиционно.**

Например, `COLD` потенциально можно представить универсальным физическим образом «snowflake/ice», а не создавать абстрактный знак.

---

# **4\. Главная грамматическая единица: frame**

Вместо предложения я предлагаю **frame**.

Frame представляет один практический смысл.

Например:

\[ WATER \]

Просто вода.

\[ COLD  
  WATER \]

Холодная вода.

\[ 3×  
  BOTTLE  
  WATER \]

Три бутылки воды.

А несколько frames образуют сообщение:

\[ YES \]   \[ COLD \+ WATER \]   \[ SALE \+ WATER \]

Это соответствует:

> Yes. Cold water. For sale.

То есть мы не пытаемся имитировать английский порядок слов.

---

# **5\. Пространство является частью грамматики**

Это, на мой взгляд, принципиально важно.

Вместо большого количества грамматических символов используем пространственные отношения.

### **Рядом**

Два независимых связанных понятия:

`WATER SALE`

### **Внутри**

X является содержимым/частью Y:

\[BOTTLE  
   WATER\]

То есть water-in-bottle.

### **Над объектом**

Модификатор:

COLD  
 WATER

### **Перед объектом**

Количество:

•••  BOTTLE

### **Стрелка между объектами**

Направленное отношение:

PERSON → PLACE

Это позволяет человеку буквально «читать картинку».

Для машины те же отношения представляются как дерево, например:

entity: bottle  
content: water  
quantity: 3  
property: cold  
---

# **6\. Composition вместо новых слов**

Это центральный механизм всей системы.

Допустим, Core содержит:

`PERSON`

`VEHICLE`

`MONEY`

`MOVE`

Тогда вместо отдельных базовых символов для taxi, bus driver, passenger, payment и т. д. мы сначала пытаемся строить понятия.

Например:

PERSON \+ VEHICLE

ещё неоднозначно.

Но:

PERSON → VEHICLE

может означать «сесть/ехать на транспортном средстве».

А:

MONEY → PERSON

может означать передачу денег / оплату.

При этом есть важный предел: композиция не должна превращаться в ребус.

Если `TAXI` постоянно нужен в реальной коммуникации и его композиционное представление регулярно понимают неправильно, он становится кандидатом в контролируемый словарь.

То есть принцип:

> **compose first, lexicalize only when empirical evidence says composition fails.**

---

# **7\. Порядок чтения**

Для человека нужен предсказуемый порядок.

Я бы выбрал:

**слева направо для последовательности событий**,  
**центр → наружу для структуры одного понятия**.

Например:

PERSON → VEHICLE → STATION

читается как движение человека на транспорте к станции.

Но:

  COLD  
\[ WATER \]

читается от центрального `WATER`, после чего применяется свойство `COLD`.

Это особенно полезно машине: сообщение является не строкой токенов, а небольшим графом.

---

# **8\. Один символ \= одно семантическое ядро**

Нельзя делать символ, который одновременно означает:

> купить / магазин / продаётся / цена.

Это очень привлекательная ошибка при проектировании пиктограмм.

Вместо этого:

`MONEY`

`TRANSFER`

`OBJECT`

дают разные конструкции.

Например:

MONEY → OBJECT

может выражать exchange/purchase.

А:

? MONEY : OBJECT

может выражать price query.

Точные операторы мы определим в следующей части.

---

# **9\. Iconicity hierarchy**

Не все понятия одинаково легко визуализируются.

Поэтому я бы ввёл четыре уровня.

**Level 1: direct icon**

Вода → капля.

Человек → человеческая фигура.

Бутылка → силуэт бутылки.

**Level 2: conventional physical metaphor**

Cold → snowflake.

Danger → warning triangle / salient hazard shape.

Direction → arrow.

**Level 3: composition**

Payment:

`MONEY → RECEIVER`

Card payment:

`CARD → PAYMENT`

**Level 4: arbitrary identifier**

То, что невозможно разумно показать:

название гостиницы, имя человека, конкретный поезд, Wi-Fi network и т. д.

Именно Level 4 требует отдельного механизма external reference, который разберём позже.

---

# **10\. Guessability важнее красоты**

Каждый Core symbol должен проходить мысленный тест:

> Если показать этот символ человеку из другой языковой и культурной среды без легенды, насколько вероятно, что он хотя бы попадёт в правильную семантическую область?

Не обязательно угадать точное слово.

Например, капля может интерпретироваться как:

water / liquid / drink.

Для нашего протокола это приемлемая первичная ошибка.

А красивый абстрактный знак, который после обучения идеально означает WATER, хуже.

Поэтому критерий:

**semantic neighbourhood recognition \> exact lexical recognition.**

---

# **11\. Redundancy разрешена**

Обычные языки стремятся к экономии.

Здесь иногда выгоднее повторить информацию.

Например, опасность:

\!   DANGER   ¬ → PLACE

Даже если человек не понял `DANGER`, он может понять:

**warning \+ no \+ movement \+ place**

То есть «туда не идти».

Это intentional redundancy.

Она особенно полезна в emergency communication.

---

# **12\. Ошибка должна деградировать безопасно**

Это ещё один фундаментальный принцип.

Если сообщение невозможно понять полностью, желательно сохранить наиболее практически важную часть.

Например:

> This bridge may collapse. Do not enter.

Если `bridge`, `collapse` или `may` отсутствуют:

\!   DANGER  
¬ PERSON → PLACE

всё равно передаёт:

> DANGER. DO NOT GO THERE.

Это лучше, чем пытаться точно кодировать исходную фразу и получить совершенно непонятный ребус.

Я бы назвал это **semantic graceful degradation**.

---

## **Получившаяся архитектура**

Таким образом, пока система состоит всего из:

ENTITY  
PROPERTY  
ACTION  
OPERATOR

которые образуют:

FRAME

а frames образуют:

MESSAGE

При этом:

symbol  
   ↓  
composition  
   ↓  
frame  
   ↓  
message

и один и тот же underlying semantic graph может быть прочитан человеком визуально и машиной структурно.

Самое важное решение здесь: **мы не строим визуальный аналог английского или любого другого естественного языка**. Мы строим ограниченный semantic protocol.

Следующий логический слой уже гораздо интереснее: определить очень маленькую грамматику операторов для **question, yes/no, negation, need/want, quantity, direction, action, possession/availability, quality и exchange**. Именно там станет понятно, насколько маленьким действительно может быть Core.

Тогда переходим к самой важной части: минимальной грамматике. Я немного ужесточу предыдущую архитектуру, потому что уже видно потенциальную проблему: если разрешить слишком много способов пространственной композиции, человек без обучения не будет знать, что означает «сверху», «внутри» или «рядом». Поэтому пространственная грамматика должна быть очень ограниченной и повторяемой. Это согласуется и с общими принципами визуальной коммуникации: proximity, hierarchy и sequence действительно хорошо передают отношения, но работают лучше при последовательном применении. [IEEE Professional Communication Society](https://procomm.ieee.org/elements-of-visual-communication/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## **Часть 2\. Минимальная грамматика**

Я предлагаю всего **7 универсальных операций**. Всё остальное по возможности строить из них.

### **1\. ENTITY \+ PROPERTY**

Свойство помещается непосредственно над объектом:

❄  
 💧

\= cold water.

↑  
 BOX

\= high/up object, если контекст это допускает.

Главное правило:

> Верхний маленький знак модифицирует нижний крупный знак.

Не нужно создавать `COLD-WATER`, `HOT-WATER`, `COLD-FOOD` и т. д.

Это обычная композиция:

PROPERTY  
   ↓  
ENTITY  
---

### **2\. X → Y**

Стрелка является самым мощным отношением системы.

Её базовая семантика:

> **направленное изменение/передача/движение от X к Y.**

Например:

PERSON → PLACE

человек движется туда.

MONEY → PERSON

деньги передаются человеку.

OBJECT → PERSON

объект передаётся человеку.

Контекст уточняет разновидность действия.

Это сознательная полисемия. Нам не нужны отдельные Core-глаголы `give`, `travel`, `send`, `deliver`, если практическое сообщение понятно через направление отношения.

---

### **3\. NEGATION**

Для отрицания нужен один чрезвычайно заметный оператор.

Лучший визуальный кандидат:

╱

диагональная черта поверх конструкции.

Например:

🚶 → 🚪  
   ╱

\= не входить / не идти туда.

Ключевое правило:

> Negation применяется ко всему frame, через который проходит slash.

Это гораздо лучше отдельного символа `NO`, потому что scope отрицания становится видимым.

Например:

╱ \[SMOKE\]

не курить.

Но:

╱ \[PERSON → PLACE\]

не входить.

Один оператор, две конструкции.

---

## **4\. QUESTION**

Здесь возникает первая действительно сложная проблема.

Знак `?` великолепно узнаваем во многих культурах, но он происходит из письменности. Мы поставили условие «без написанных слов и alphabetic abbreviations», но не запретили punctuation.

Тем не менее я бы **не использовал `?` в фундаментальном Core**.

Нам нужен чисто визуальный оператор.

Предлагаю:

◇

пустой ромб \= **unknown / requested information**.

То есть вопрос не является отдельным типом предложения.

**Вопрос \= frame с неизвестным элементом.**

Например:

TOILET → ◇

означает:

> toilet → unknown place

Практическая интерпретация:

> Where is the toilet?

Цена:

OBJECT ⇄ ◇ MONEY

\= object exchanges for unknown quantity of money.

То есть:

> How much?

Это очень сильное решение, потому что один `UNKNOWN` заменяет:

where?  
what?  
which?  
how many?  
how much?

Позиция неизвестного показывает, **что именно спрашивается**.

---

# **5\. YES / NO**

Здесь я бы не смешивал affirmation и negation.

Negation уже существует как оператор `/`.

Но ответ «нет» означает отрицание предыдущего frame:

╱ ↶

где `↶` условно означает previous frame.

Это слишком абстрактно.

Для реального разговора лучше разрешить два dialog primitives:

**YES:** крупная галочка/check-like mark.

**NO:** diagonal slash.

Почему отдельный YES оправдан?

Потому что:

> Yes.

является чрезвычайно частым самостоятельным коммуникативным актом и плохо раскладывается на физические сущности.

То есть Core допускает небольшое число **social/dialogue primitives**.

Получаем:

✓

\= yes / correct / available / confirmed

и

╱

\= no / not / prohibited

Точное значение определяется frame.

---

# **6\. Quantity**

Количество должно модифицировать объект, а не существовать отдельно где\-то в сообщении.

Например:

••• × \[BOTTLE\]

\= three bottles.

Но `×` здесь, возможно, вообще лишний.

Можно установить правило:

> Повторяющийся quantity marker непосредственно перед entity означает количество entity.

••• BOTTLE

Три бутылки.

Однако буквально рисовать три бутылки:

BOTTLE BOTTLE BOTTLE

плохо масштабируется.

Поэтому нужен отдельный числовой механизм. К нему вернёмся в следующей части.

Семантически конструкция просто:

\[QUANTITY\] \[ENTITY\]  
---

# **7\. Direction**

Direction не требует слов `left`, `right`, `up`, `down`.

Стрелка сама несёт геометрию:

←  
→  
↑  
↓  
↖  
↗  
↙  
↘

Например:

TOILET →

\= toilet that way.

Это один из редких случаев, где изображение передаёт информацию практически напрямую, без символического перевода.

Особенно полезно, что машина тоже получает буквально вектор:

direction \= east/right

или в физическом пространстве:

heading \= 90°

если устройство знает ориентацию.

---

# **8\. ACTION**

Здесь я бы существенно отличался от традиционного pictogram dictionary.

Не нужно иметь:

`GO`  
`COME`  
`GIVE`  
`TAKE`  
`BUY`  
`SELL`  
`PAY`

как семь независимых primitives.

Большую часть можно получить через **отношения**.

Например:

PERSON → TAXI

может означать enter/use taxi.

TAXI → STATION

taxi goes to station.

MONEY → SELLER

payment.

GOODS → BUYER

goods transfer.

А вместе:

MONEY → SELLER  
GOODS → BUYER

это exchange.

То есть действие часто является **геометрией изменения**, а не отдельным глаголом.

Это радикально уменьшает Core.

---

# **9\. NEED / WANT**

Но тут возникает случай, который стрелкой нормально не выразить:

> I need water.

Если:

PERSON → WATER

это может означать «иду к воде».

Поэтому нужен оператор **desired/needed state**.

Я бы не разделял WANT и NEED в Core.

Практический протокол обычно требует:

> человек хочет получить X.

Предлагаю визуальную метафору:

PERSON … \[empty slot shaped for X\] … X

Но это трудно стандартизировать.

Более надёжный вариант: **рука, протянутая к объекту**.

Семантически:

REQUEST(X)

Визуально:

hand → X

Получается универсальное:

HAND → WATER

\= give/request water.

А:

PERSON \+ HAND → TAXI

\= I need/request taxi.

Таким образом, отдельного абстрактного `NEED` может вообще не быть.

---

# **10\. HAVE / AVAILABLE**

Теперь:

> Do you have water?

Это не совсем `REQUEST(WATER)`.

Человек спрашивает о наличии.

Можно представить:

◇ \[WATER\]

но это слишком широко: «что с водой?»

Лучше ввести очень простое отношение **PRESENT / HERE**.

Визуальная метафора:

**объект внутри открытой рамки/локального пространства.**

┌─────┐  
│ 💧  │  
└─────┘

\= water here / water present.

Тогда:

◇ \[ WATER HERE \]

\= Is water here/available?

Но это уже второй смысл `◇`: не unknown slot, а question marker.

Я бы этого **не допускал**.

Лучше:

\[ WATER \] \= ◇

где unknown занимает состояние availability.

На semantic layer:

availability(WATER) \= UNKNOWN

Это визуально ещё надо хорошо решить, но логика чистая.

---

# **11\. SALE / BUY**

Здесь появляется интересная композиция.

Вместо символа SALE:

OBJECT ⇄ MONEY

\= object exchanged for money.

Это значит:

**for sale / buy / purchase**, в зависимости от коммуникативной позиции.

Например:

WATER ⇄ MONEY

\= water is commercially available.

А:

WATER ⇄ ◇ MONEY

\= price of water?

Мы получили `SALE`, `BUY` и `PRICE` вообще без отдельных символов.

Это именно тот тип композиционности, который нам нужен.

---

# **12\. CARD PAYMENT**

Если есть entity:

CARD

то:

CARD → MONEY

не совсем корректно: карта не превращается в деньги.

Лучше иметь общий **PAYMENT/EXCHANGE relation**:

CARD ⇄ GOODS

\= card-for-goods exchange.

Но человеку это может быть неочевидно.

Поэтому можно использовать:

CARD  
  ↓  
MONEY

где CARD является property/method для MONEY.

То есть:

**card-mediated money/payment.**

Вопрос:

◇ \[ CARD \+ MONEY → SELLER \]

\= card payment possible?

Это пример того, где composition требует проверки на реальных людях.

---

# **13\. QUALITY**

Quality всегда прикрепляется к entity.

Например:

❄  
 💧

cold water.

🔥  
 FOOD

hot food.

\+  
 HOUSE

может быть dangerous territory, потому что `+` имеет слишком много значений.

Поэтому qualities должны быть либо:

1. физически иконическими;  
2. очень небольшим закрытым набором;  
3. либо external vocabulary.

Не нужно пытаться кодировать:

beautiful, interesting, delicious, comfortable, elegant...

в Core.

---

# **14\. WARNING**

Здесь нужен специальный pragmatic operator.

Я бы использовал не `!`, а **треугольную рамку**:

 /\\  
 / X\\  
/\_\_\_\_\\

Всё внутри неё означает:

> ATTENTION / HAZARD concerning X.

Треугольная warning-форма уже широко используется в signage, поэтому шанс распознавания высокий.

Например:

△ \[FIRE\]

\= fire danger.

И:

△ \[PLACE\]

╱ PERSON → PLACE

передаёт:

> Danger here. Do not enter.

Важно: warning и prohibition остаются разными.

---

# **Итого: грамматическое ядро получается удивительно маленьким**

Пока нам фактически нужны только такие операции:

| Operation | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| `A + B` | conceptual composition |
| `PROPERTY / A` | property of A |
| `A → B` | directed movement/transfer |
| `A ⇄ B` | exchange |
| `/ FRAME` | negation/prohibition |
| `◇` | unknown slot |
| `△ FRAME` | warning |
| `Q × A` | quantity of A |

Плюс `✓` как dialog-level **affirm/confirm**.

Это уже позволяет выразить довольно много вещей без сотен «слов».

Но здесь обнаружилась важная вещь, которую я бы зафиксировал как принцип системы:

> **Question не должен быть знаком вопроса. Question возникает из наличия неизвестного узла в semantic graph.**

Например:

TOILET → ◇

**Where is the toilet?**

WATER ⇄ ◇ MONEY

**How much is the water?**

◇ × BOTTLE

**How many bottles?**

◇ → STATION

**What goes to the station?**

Это очень компактно и одновременно хорошо для машин: `◇` буквально является переменной.

Следующий шаг я бы посвятил **числам и reference system**, потому что именно здесь легко испортить всю систему. Нам нужно решить, как без арабских цифр передавать `1, 2, 3, 6, 20, 150`, деньги, время, номера автобусов и собственные имена, при этом не превращая Core в альтернативную письменность. После этого уже можно окончательно собрать минимальный словарь.

Продолжим с числами и механизмом для того, чего нет в Core. Здесь я бы внёс одно принципиальное изменение относительно предыдущего наброска: системе не стоит запрещать повторение символов. Для малых чисел оно как раз может быть самым понятным способом.

## **Часть 3\. Numbers, references и границы языка**

### **1\. Числа: две разные задачи**

Есть огромная разница между:

> three bottles

и:

> bus 742

В первом случае число несёт **quantity**. Во втором это **identifier**.

Не стоит заставлять один механизм одинаково обслуживать оба случая.

Для Core нам прежде всего нужны количества.

### **2\. Малые количества: точки**

Для 1–4 я бы использовал непосредственно количество одинаковых marks:

•       one  
••      two  
•••     three

••  
••      four

Причём после двух лучше переходить от линии к компактной геометрической группе. Человек воспринимает малые количества почти мгновенно, а не считает элементы по одному.

Тогда:

•••  \[BOTTLE\]

\= three bottles.

Никакой отдельный знак `3` изучать не требуется.

Это сильнее придуманной цифры именно для нашего требования **little or no prior training**.

---

## **3\. После четырёх нужен группирующий принцип**

Шесть точек подряд:

••••••

уже начинают требовать счёта.

Поэтому нужен визуально различимый блок **five**.

Например:

••  
 •  
••

пять точек в стабильной конфигурации.

Тогда 6:

••  
 •   •  
••

то есть:

**recognizable five-cluster \+ one**.

7 \= 5 \+ 2\.

8 \= 5 \+ 3\.

9 \= 5 \+ 4\.

10 \= два five-clusters.

Это существенно лучше, чем придумывать десять новых glyphs.

У системы фактически остаётся только один learned convention:

> эта стабильная группа означает полный блок из пяти.

Причём даже без знания правила её всё ещё можно буквально пересчитать.

---

## **4\. Большие числа**

Здесь я бы поставил жёсткую границу.

Не нужно превращать Core в полноценную систему счисления.

Для практического visual protocol наиболее часты:

1 person  
2 tickets  
3 bottles  
4 bags  
6 euros  
10 minutes

Но попытка визуально записать:

> 18,742

точками бессмысленна.

Поэтому после определённого порога включается **numeric extension layer**.

Например, Core гарантирует native quantities `0–10`.

Всё большее является external representation.

Это может быть:

* локальная привычная цифровая запись;  
* экран калькулятора;  
* QR/data payload;  
* machine-readable numeric field;  
* физический объект, например цена на кассе.

Это не недостаток системы. Это сознательное разделение ответственности.

---

# **5\. Zero**

Zero интересен.

Пустота сама по себе плохо работает как символ: невозможно отличить intentional zero от отсутствующего значения.

Поэтому zero должен быть:

○

пустой quantity container.

В контексте количества:

○ PERSON

\= zero people / nobody.

Но я пока не включал бы его в самый минимальный Core. В большинстве практических сообщений negation решает ту же задачу:

╱ WATER

\= no water.

---

# **6\. Деньги**

Нам совершенно не нужен Core-symbol для euro, dollar, yen и т. д.

Нужен один концепт:

**MONEY / VALUE**

Визуальная метафора может быть монетой:

◉

Но простой круг слишком абстрактен.

Я бы нарисовал **две перекрывающиеся монеты**:

◯  
 ◯

Это физический объект, ассоциируемый с деньгами во множестве культур.

Тогда:

••  
 •   •   MONEY  
••

\= six monetary units.

Именно **units**, не dollars/euros.

Это соответствует практической ситуации:

> Six units of local currency.

Контекст страны/магазина определяет валюту.

---

# **7\. Currency как context**

Это важный общий принцип:

> Не кодировать информацию, которая уже однозначно определяется физическим контекстом.

В кафе во Франции:

6 MONEY

практически означает €6.

В Японии:

600 MONEY

будет интерпретировано в yen.

Если валюта действительно неоднозначна, например международная торговля, используется external identifier.

Так Core не получает десятки валют.

---

# **8\. Proper names вообще не должны становиться vocabulary**

Предположим, мне нужно:

> Hotel Negresco

Создавать символ `NEGRESCO` нельзя.

То же касается:

Paris  
Anton  
Ryanair  
iPhone  
train 6172  
Gate B17.

Для этого нужен **REFERENCE container**.

Предлагаю особую рамку:

┌ · · · ┐  
   X  
└ · · · ┘

Её значение:

> Это внешний идентификатор. Не пытайся семантически интерпретировать содержимое как Core.

Внутри может находиться:

* написанное человеком название;  
* логотип;  
* фотография;  
* карта;  
* QR;  
* NFC reference;  
* machine ID;  
* номер;  
* лицо человека.

Например концептуально:

\[PLACE\] \+ REF\[Hotel Negresco\]

означает конкретное место.

Это очень важное разграничение:

**Core communicates meaning.**  
**Reference identifies things.**

---

# **9\. Незнакомые понятия**

Представим, что Core не знает слова `avocado`.

Можно показать изображение avocado в reference frame:

REF\[🥑\]

И использовать его:

REF\[avocado\] ⇄ MONEY

\= avocado for sale.

Это не добавляет AVOCADO в язык.

Система просто говорит:

> этот конкретно указанный объект обменивается на деньги.

То же самое прекрасно работает для машины. Reference может содержать:

external-id: 981273

при сохранении той же визуальной конструкции для человека.

---

# **10\. Unknown и Reference принципиально различаются**

Получаем два очень полезных контейнера:

◇

\= UNKNOWN

и:

REF\[X\]

\= KNOWN EXTERNAL THING X.

Например:

TOILET → ◇

\= Where is the toilet?

Но:

PERSON → REF\[photo of building\]

\= go to this particular building.

Это делает язык значительно мощнее практически без увеличения словаря.

---

# **11\. Как добавлять новые слова**

Здесь нужен довольно жёсткий governance rule.

Новый Core primitive нельзя добавлять просто потому, что:

> было бы удобно иметь отдельную иконку.

Иначе через год будет 4 000 символов.

Я бы установил четыре последовательных теста.

**Test 1: Frequency**

Понятие регулярно требуется в практической коммуникации?

Если нет → external/reference vocabulary.

**Test 2: Composition**

Можно ли достаточно понятно выразить его существующими primitives?

Если да → нового символа нет.

**Test 3: Guessability**

Можно ли создать визуальную метафору, которую неподготовленные люди регулярно относят хотя бы к правильной semantic category?

Если нет → скорее всего не Core.

**Test 4: Compression benefit**

Сильно ли новый primitive упрощает чрезвычайно частую или критически важную композицию?

Только после прохождения этих тестов символ становится кандидатом.

---

# **12\. Core должен быть versioned**

Это уже важно для human-machine communication.

Например:

CORE 1

может иметь условно 40 primitives.

Следующая версия не должна менять значения существующих primitives.

То есть:

> once assigned, semantic identity is immutable.

Можно улучшить рисунок бутылки.

Но нельзя сделать так, чтобы semantic ID `17` раньше означал BOTTLE, а потом CUP.

Machine representation поэтому должен хранить не картинку, а примерно:

concept: vessel.bottle  
core: 1

Пиктограмма является rendering этого semantic ID.

---

# **13\. Controlled extensions**

Допустим, транспортная система постоянно нуждается в:

train  
bus  
tram  
airplane  
platform  
ticket.

Нет необходимости помещать всё это в Universal Core.

Можно иметь:

CORE  
TRANSPORT extension  
MEDICAL extension  
FOOD extension  
DIGITAL extension

Причём extension тоже проходит governance.

Получается иерархия:

Universal Core  
      ↓  
Domain vocabulary  
      ↓  
External Reference

Это хороший барьер против uncontrolled vocabulary growth.

---

# **14\. Что делать, когда точного значения нет**

Вот здесь, на мой взгляд, система должна сильно отличаться от естественного языка.

Нельзя притворяться, что сообщение точнее, чем оно есть.

Допустим, нужно сказать:

> I am allergic to peanuts.

А Core знает только:

PERSON  
BAD/HARM  
FOOD  
PEANUT reference.

Можно передать:

PERSON  
   \+  
△ \[ REF\[peanut\] → PERSON \]

Практический смысл:

> PEANUT \+ PERSON \= DANGER.

Это **не точный перевод allergy**.

Но для официанта сохранена наиболее важная информация.

Я бы ввёл правило:

> Preserve operational consequence before linguistic fidelity.

Это фундаментальная особенность протокола.

---

# **15\. Explicit approximation**

Но машине полезно знать, что значение потеряно.

Поэтому semantic representation может иметь:

precision: approximate

Для человека можно использовать специальную **неполную/разомкнутую рамку** вокруг frame:

┌────  
│ symbols  
└────

\= approximate / incomplete meaning.

Но я пока **не включал бы этот знак в Core**.

Почему?

Потому что неподготовленный человек никогда сам не догадается, что разорванная рамка означает semantic approximation.

Это хороший machine metadata, но плохой zero-training human symbol.

И это приводит к важному выводу.

---

# **16\. Human и machine versions не должны быть абсолютно одинаковыми**

Они должны иметь **одну семантическую модель**, но разные representations.

Человек видит:

•••  \[BOTTLE\]  
      \[WATER\]

Машина получает что-то вроде:

frame:  
  entity: bottle  
  content: water  
  quantity: 3

Человеку не нужны UUID, confidence scores и ontology IDs.

Машине они очень полезны:

concept\_id  
relation  
scope  
quantity  
confidence  
extension  
version  
reference

Таким образом:

**одинаковое meaning graph, разные renderers.**

Это гораздо разумнее попытки заставить computer vision угадывать смысл картинки.

---

# **17\. Machine layer не должен зависеть от распознавания пиктограмм**

Если сообщение цифровое, картинка и semantic payload должны передаваться вместе.

Например:

VISUAL:  
••• BOTTLE/WATER

SEMANTIC:  
quantity(3,  
  bottle(  
    content=water  
  )  
)

Если человек сфотографировал бумажный знак, машина действительно использует vision recognition.

Но если сообщение было создано цифровой системой, заставлять AI повторно распознавать собственную картинку бессмысленно.

---

## **Теперь можно сформулировать архитектуру целиком**

Получается четыре уровня:

MEANING GRAPH  
      ↓  
UNIVERSAL CORE  
      ↓  
DOMAIN EXTENSIONS  
      ↓  
EXTERNAL REFERENCES

и два способа rendering:

            MEANING  
             /     \\  
            /       \\  
       HUMAN         MACHINE  
      visual         structured

Это решает довольно большую часть первоначальной задачи без огромного словаря.

И я бы здесь придерживался особенно строгого правила: **пространственная композиция должна быть ограничена фиксированными отношениями**, потому что proximity, hierarchy и sequence действительно способны передавать структуру, но произвольное использование расположения быстро создаёт неоднозначность. [IEEE Professional Communication Society](https://procomm.ieee.org/elements-of-visual-communication/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Следующий шаг теперь самый предметный: **собрать настоящий Universal Core**. Я бы попробовал удержать его примерно в **30–40 primitives**, причём для каждого проверить: нужен ли он вообще, нельзя ли его получить композицией, какой должна быть визуальная метафора и насколько реально понять её без обучения. После этого у нас уже будет достаточно материала, чтобы закодировать все семь исходных сообщений и увидеть слабые места системы.

Да. Теперь соберём **Core v0.1**. И здесь я бы был ещё строже: не ставил цель набрать 30–40 знаков. Если система работает с 20–25, это лучше.

Небольшая методологическая оговорка: сам дизайн ниже остаётся нашим независимым проектированием. Я лишь проверил один внешний принцип, важный для дальнейшего тестирования: ISO действительно разделяет две вещи, которые нам тоже стоит измерять отдельно, способность человека **распознать элементы изображения** и способность **понять предполагаемое значение символа**. [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/59226.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## **Часть 4\. Universal Core v0.1**

Я предлагаю начать всего с **24 primitives**.

Причём primitive здесь означает не обязательно «слово». Это может быть entity, operator или pragmatic marker.

### **A. Physical entities: 10**

| Primitive | Семантика | Визуальная метафора | Почему может читаться без обучения |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| PERSON | человек / люди | простая человеческая фигура | узнаваемый силуэт |
| WATER | вода / питьевая жидкость | крупная капля | физическая ассоциация |
| FOOD | еда | миска \+ ложка | ситуация питания |
| TOILET | туалет | унитаз сбоку | изображает сам объект, а не WC |
| PLACE | место / пункт назначения | контур location-like enclosure с точкой | объект находится в обозначенном месте |
| SHELTER | помещение / здание | крыша над пространством | физическое укрытие |
| VEHICLE | наземный транспорт | профиль четырёхколёсного средства | узнаваемая категория |
| BOTTLE | бутылка / ёмкость | характерный силуэт бутылки | прямое изображение |

| MONEY | деньги / стоимость | две перекрывающиеся монеты | физическая денежная метафора |

Я сознательно **не добавляю**:

car, bus, taxi, train, cup, restaurant, hotel, shop, airport...

Пока это extensions или compositions.

---

## **B. Physical/action concepts: 4**

### **HAND / REQUEST**

Визуально: открытая протянутая ладонь.

Семантическое ядро:

> request / receive / give me

Например:

HAND → WATER

≈ water, please / give me water.

Это одновременно избавляет нас от отдельного `PLEASE`.

---

### **EYE / SEE**

Глаз.

Семантика:

> see / look / visible.

Он пригодится для navigation, interfaces и warnings.

---

### **MOVE**

Фигура/объект \+ motion trace.

Но здесь возникает вопрос: нужен ли MOVE вообще?

У нас уже есть:

PERSON → PLACE

которое естественно читается как движение.

Поэтому я бы **удалил MOVE из Core**.

Это хороший пример того, как должен происходить pruning.

---

### **STOP**

Ладонь, обращённая к наблюдателю.

Семантика:

> stop / halt.

Она полезна именно как safety primitive.

---

Таким образом, здесь пока только **3**, не 4\.

Core \= 13\.

---

# **C. Physical qualities: 4**

Здесь особенно опасно начать создавать визуальный словарь прилагательных.

Оставим только практически важные физические состояния.

### **COLD**

Снежинка.

❄  
 💧

cold water.

Почему: snow/ice → low temperature.

---

### **HOT**

Три восходящие волнистые линии.

Не огонь.

Огонь означает одновременно:

fire / burning / hot / danger.

Пар над объектом гораздо лучше передаёт именно температуру.

≈≈≈  
FOOD

hot food.

---

### **BIG**

Объект с outward markers:

← \[X\] →

\= large / more / expanded.

### **SMALL**

Обратная конструкция:

→ \[X\] ←

\= small / less / compressed.

Но я бы пока **не включал BIG/SMALL в Core**.

Они полезны, но не нужны нашим базовым сценариям.

Получаем всего:

**COLD, HOT.**

Core \= 15\.

---

# **D. Relations/operators: 6**

Вот здесь находится настоящий язык.

### **1\. DIRECTION / TRANSFER**

→

Семантика:

> directed relation from A to B.

PERSON → PLACE

person moves to place.

MONEY → PERSON

money transferred to person.

---

### **2\. EXCHANGE**

⇄

Семантика:

> reciprocal transfer / exchange.

WATER ⇄ MONEY

water exchanged for money.

То есть:

> water for sale.

Это очень ценный primitive: из него появляются commerce, sale, purchase и price.

---

### **3\. NEGATION**

Диагональная черта поверх frame.

╱ \[ X \]

\= not X.

Для action:

╱ \[PERSON → PLACE\]

### **1\. Symbols likely to be understood immediately**

The strongest symbols are concrete objects and physically motivated signs.

| Symbol/concept | Likely blind interpretation | Risk |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| PERSON | person/human | Low |
| WATER, drop | water/liquid/drop | Low |
| BOTTLE | bottle/container | Low |
| TOILET, actual toilet silhouette | toilet | Low |
| VEHICLE | car/vehicle | Low-medium |
| CARD | card/bank card/ID | Medium |
| FOOD, bowl \+ utensil | food/eating/restaurant | Medium |
| COLD, snowflake | cold/snow/ice | Low |
| HOT, rising waves | hot/steam/smell | Medium |
| → | direction/go/next | Low |
| diagonal slash | no/prohibited | Low |
| warning triangle | warning/danger | Low |
| STOP hand | stop/do not proceed | Low |
| 1–4 dots | quantity | Low if attached clearly |

These are the strongest part of the proposal because the image itself carries much of the semantic information.

Even here, exact meanings are less reliable than semantic neighbourhoods. A drop may mean "water", "liquid", "wet", or "drink". A bowl may mean "food", "restaurant", "soup", or "eat". For the intended protocol that is often acceptable, but it matters when symbols are composed.

### **2\. Symbols likely to require explicit learning**

Several supposedly "primitive" elements are not remotely self-explanatory.

The worst are:

`◇ = UNKNOWN`

`⇄ = EXCHANGE`

`✓ = AFFIRM`

`PRESENT(X)`

`HAND = REQUEST`

`PLACE`

and probably the external-reference frame.

A novice seeing:

TOILET → ◇

might interpret it as:

"toilet leads to diamond"

"toilet this direction"

"toilet destination"

"move something from toilet"

There is nothing inherently interrogative about an empty diamond.

Likewise:

WATER ⇄ MONEY

could mean:

"water and money"

"convert water into money"

"water costs money"

"buy water"

"refund"

"water vending"

The intended `for sale` interpretation depends on learning the grammar.

So one uncomfortable conclusion is:

> Core v0.1 is not actually a zero-training language. It is a small learnable language containing some highly guessable vocabulary.

That distinction matters.

### **3\. Dangerous ambiguities**

Some ambiguity is merely inconvenient. Some could produce the opposite practical action.

Consider:

PERSON → PLACE

I defined `→` broadly as directed relation/movement/transfer.

A novice could reasonably read this as:

"go there"

"entrance"

"person comes from here"

"people this way"

"destination"

"send person there"

This becomes safety-critical when negated:

╱ \[PERSON → PLACE\]

I intend "do not enter".

Someone might understand "no pedestrians", "no exit", "people cannot go there", or even simply "no people".

Those are related, but not identical.

Another dangerous example is the hand. An open hand can mean:

stop, give, receive, help, request, five, hand, touch, don't touch.

Yet I proposed both `STOP hand` and `HAND/REQUEST`. Distinguishing two different hand configurations reliably across drawing styles and tiny sizes is risky.

### **4\. Overloaded concepts**

The most overloaded primitive is `→`.

I assigned it:

* physical direction;  
* movement;  
* transfer;  
* semantic relation;  
* destination.

Those are not the same operation.

For example:

PERSON → TAXI  
MONEY → PERSON  
TOILET → ◇

The arrows respectively mean approximately:

"enter/use"

"give/pay"

"is located at"

The reader is doing a huge amount of contextual inference.

`⇄` is similarly overloaded:

exchange, commerce, buying, selling, reciprocal transfer.

HAND is overloaded between physical hand and pragmatic REQUEST.

MONEY is potentially overloaded between:

money, price, cost, payment, monetary value.

PLACE risks meaning:

location, destination, here, building, area.

PRESENT is particularly problematic because it tries to cover:

exists, available, here, has, can use.

For example, "Do you have water?" and "Can I pay by card?" are semantically different predicates, yet I was trying to solve both through `PRESENT`.

That is a warning sign.

### **5\. The grammar may be much too abstract**

The proposal assumes an untrained observer will discover rules such as:

* small symbol above another symbol \= property;  
* symbol inside another \= semantic containment;  
* slash scope is determined by the frame;  
* dots preceding an object quantify it;  
* arrow connects semantic arguments;  
* diamond represents an unbound variable;  
* reciprocal arrow means exchange;  
* separate frames form discourse sequence.

A linguist or programmer can learn this very quickly.

A tourist looking at a sign for five seconds cannot be expected to infer it.

There is an important difference between:

> easy to learn

and:

> easy to infer without learning.

I blurred those two requirements in the initial design.

### **6\. Scope is insufficiently defined**

Consider:

COLD  
WATER ⇄ MONEY

Does COLD modify:

* WATER;  
* the whole transaction;  
* MONEY;  
* availability?

Visually this can be solved with spacing, but spacing is a weak grammatical delimiter.

Similarly:

╱ PERSON → PLACE

Does slash mean:

`NOT(person) → place`

or:

`NOT(person → place)`?

Frames help, but now frame boundaries themselves become grammatical punctuation that must be learned.

Nested compositions make this worse.

### **7\. Numerical notation has significant problems**

The 1–4 dot system is strong.

After that it deteriorates quickly.

My proposed five-dot cluster has several problems.

First, a novice has no reason to know whether:

••  
 •  
••

means five, a decorative shape, or a distinct symbol.

Second, 6 \= five-cluster \+ one requires the reader to understand grouping.

Third, once we reach values such as 17, 28, 60 or 150, the system collapses into either visual clutter or a learned positional notation.

Fourth, there is no clean treatment yet for:

* decimals;  
* fractions;  
* negative numbers;  
* time;  
* dates;  
* phone numbers;  
* platform numbers;  
* bus numbers;  
* prices such as 4.50;  
* measurement units.

The earlier decision to offload large numbers to an external numeric layer is sensible architecturally, but it means the visual protocol itself has an abrupt capability boundary.

There is another machine problem: six dots representing quantity and six unrelated circular features in an icon must be reliably distinguished.

### **8\. Composition can become absurdly long**

The taxi example already exposes this:

HAND → \[VEHICLE ⇄ MONEY\]

for:

> I need a taxi.

We compressed the vocabulary but increased decoding effort.

"Three bottles of cold water, please" becomes conceptually something like:

REQUEST →  
QUANTITY(3,  
    BOTTLE(  
        CONTENT:  
            COLD(WATER)  
    )  
)

Visually, that's several nested relationships for a sentence a dedicated WATER-BOTTLE symbol could communicate much faster.

The extreme version is compositional purism: avoiding new vocabulary at all costs creates visual rebuses.

So Core size and message complexity are in direct tension.

### **9\. Composition can produce meanings that are too broad**

The taxi example is especially revealing.

VEHICLE ⇄ MONEY

doesn't actually mean taxi.

It could mean:

* rent a car;  
* buy a car;  
* paid transport;  
* car dealership;  
* parking fee;  
* toll;  
* vehicle for sale.

Adding REQUEST does not fix that:

HAND → \[VEHICLE ⇄ MONEY\]

could plausibly mean:

"I want to rent a car."

That is not a minor linguistic difference. It could cause the wrong service to be provided.

So my earlier claim that this adequately preserves "I need a taxi" was too optimistic.

### **10\. Cultural assumptions remain substantial**

Several metaphors are culturally or technologically contingent.

The warning triangle is based on established signage conventions.

A check mark does not universally mean exactly "yes". Depending on context and convention, marks used for correctness, selection and cancellation vary.

A payment card with a magnetic stripe may already be technologically dated in some contexts.

Coins for MONEY assume physical currency as the metaphor for monetary value.

A snowflake for COLD depends on familiarity with the conventional stylisation of a snow crystal.

A western-style toilet silhouette may be less universal than it appears.

A bowl \+ utensil can imply a particular eating culture.

Arrows themselves are highly widespread, but their interpretation still depends partly on learned graphical convention.

So "no written language" does not equal "culture-free".

There probably is no genuinely culture-free visual vocabulary.

### **11\. The system assumes pictorial literacy**

An even deeper assumption is that simplified line drawings are transparently understood as representations.

A stylised toilet is not a toilet. It is a culturally developed 2D representation of one.

Similarly, interpreting:

small snowflake  
above  
large drop

as

> property(COLD, WATER)

requires understanding diagrammatic syntax, not merely recognizing the two pictures.

This is likely manageable for the intended population, but it contradicts a strong interpretation of "no prior training".

### **12\. PRESENT is currently the weakest semantic primitive**

I introduced PRESENT only after discovering that "Do you have water?" was hard to represent.

That is suspicious in itself.

`PRESENT(WATER)` could mean:

water exists  
water is here  
water is available  
you possess water  
water is in stock  
water can be supplied.

Then:

`PRESENT(CARD-PAYMENT)`

means something quite different:

card payment is accepted/possible.

We are forcing several modal and existential concepts into one primitive because the examples demand them.

This should be considered unresolved, not solved.

### **13\. YES depends heavily on conversational state**

Suppose the previous message is:

Then:

✓

But if displayed independently, ✓ can mean:

correct  
completed  
approved  
available  
selected  
safe  
OK.

Therefore AFFIRM is not self-contained.

Machines additionally need an explicit reference to what proposition is being affirmed. Humans recover that from conversational adjacency.

If messages become asynchronous or multiple questions are visible simultaneously, `✓` becomes ambiguous.

### **14\. "This" and deixis are unresolved**

"This is dangerous" was encoded as:

WARNING\[PLACE\]

But which place?

Current location?  
Area beyond the sign?  
Object beside the sign?  
Thing being pointed at?

I silently relied on physical context.

That works for a sign mounted on a door. It works much less well on a phone screen.

The language currently lacks a robust mechanism for:

HERE  
THERE  
ME  
YOU.

Those are extremely important in practical communication.

### **15\. Human identity is underspecified**

PERSON does not distinguish:

speaker  
listener  
third person  
staff member  
driver  
customer.

But:

> You give me the card.

> I give you the card.

At 24 px, many of the distinctions I proposed become fragile.

A bottle remains recognizable.

A drop remains recognizable.

* two overlapping coins;  
* card chip/stripe;  
* bowl \+ utensil;  
* steam lines;  
* hand orientation;  
* containment;  
* small modifiers above entities;  
* five-dot clusters;  
* nested frames;  
* external-reference border styles;  
* reciprocal arrows;  
* slash scope.

◇ PRESENT \[

   EXCHANGE

This reveals an architectural problem: **the language currently scales individual symbols, but not sentences.**

### **17\. Visual hierarchy can disappear at small size**

Our grammar uses:

position  
scale  
containment  
proximity  
orientation.

Those are exactly the features most vulnerable to rasterization and poor reproduction.

A photocopy, blurry photograph, low-resolution LED display or handwritten version could change grammar accidentally.

If:

ENTITY

becomes slightly misaligned, is it still modification?

If the containment outline disappears, the semantic relationship changes.

That is dangerous for machine recognition as well.

### **18\. Machine vision has different failure modes from humans**

Machines would probably recognize isolated standardized icons extremely well if trained on them.

The harder problem is parsing structure:

Which arrow connects which objects?

Does the slash apply to one symbol or the entire frame?

Is the snowflake a modifier or an independent entity?

Are three dots quantity or graphical decoration?

Does containment mean grammatical containment or merely icon design?

Where does one frame end and another begin?

A machine needs reliable segmentation before semantic parsing.

Our current proposal relies heavily on exactly the visual properties that computer vision can lose under perspective distortion, occlusion and low resolution.

### **19\. The same primitive can visually contain grammatical primitives**

This is a subtle but serious machine problem.

Imagine an icon for MONEY consisting of two circles.

Numbers are represented by dots.

PLACE might contain a dot.

CARD may contain a circular contactless symbol.

A pictogram may contain arrows as part of its drawing.

Now the parser has to distinguish:

**marks inside a lexical glyph**

from

**grammar marks surrounding a lexical glyph.**

Humans often solve this through Gestalt perception. A machine parser needs explicit graphical constraints.

We have not defined them.

### **20\. Handwriting tolerance is unknown**

If this is genuinely a practical protocol, people should be able to draw it.

Concrete symbols degrade differently.

A badly drawn drop still looks like a drop.

A badly drawn reciprocal arrow might become:

`↔`, `→`, two arrows, or simply a line.

A badly drawn diamond might look like a square or zero.

A badly drawn five-dot cluster might accidentally become four or six dots.

Therefore the most abstract and grammatical elements are also among the least robust under manual reproduction.

### **21\. There is no error detection**

Suppose one symbol is missed.

╱ PERSON → PLACE

If the slash is obscured, the message changes from:

> DO NOT ENTER

to approximately:

> GO/PEOPLE THIS WAY.

That is the worst possible failure mode.

The current protocol has essentially zero redundancy at the syntax level.

Safety messages need graceful degradation, but here degradation can invert meaning.

### **22\. External references solve semantics but violate the original experience**

`REF[X]` is architecturally convenient.

But if X is written text:

REF\[Charles de Gaulle\]

we have reintroduced natural language.

If X is a QR code, humans cannot interpret it.

If X is a photograph, machine matching becomes a different problem.

If X is a logo, recognition depends on brand knowledge.

So references are a useful escape hatch, but they should not be counted as evidence that the visual language itself can express the concept.

### **23\. The Core may be small for the wrong reason**

Twenty-two primitives sounds impressive.

But some complexity has simply moved from vocabulary into grammar.

There are two kinds of complexity:

many symbols \+ simple grammar

versus:

few symbols \+ complex composition

Core v0.1 aggressively chose the second.

For a programmer, that is attractive.

For a completely untrained human, it may be the wrong trade-off.

The meaningful metric is therefore not:

> How many primitives?

but something closer to:

> How many conventions must a novice infer before successfully completing a practical interaction?

On that metric, v0.1 is considerably larger than "22".

### **24\. Blind interpretation would probably be asymmetric**

I expect an untrained person to do reasonably well going from symbol to **broad meaning**:

`💧` → water/liquid  
`❄ + 💧` → cold water  
`toilet + →` → toilet that way  
`warning + slash` → danger/prohibition

But much worse going from intended sentence to **exact interpretation**:

`WATER ⇄ MONEY` → specifically "for sale"  
`◇` → interrogative variable  
`HAND → X` → specifically "I need X"  
`PRESENT(X)` → "Do you have X?"  
`VEHICLE ⇄ MONEY` → taxi.

So the protocol is currently much better at communicating semantic neighbourhoods than precise propositions.

For some use cases that is fine. For commerce, safety and human-machine commands, sometimes it isn't.

The central weakness of v0.1 is therefore quite clear: **the concrete pictograms are mostly intuitive, but I tried to obtain too much expressive power from a tiny set of abstract relational operators.** The result looks elegant as a semantic graph, but a semantic graph and a zero-training human protocol are not the same thing.

I would freeze the design here rather than fix anything yet. This critique gives us a useful baseline for the next stage: any subsequent redesign should be demonstrably better against these failure modes rather than merely prettier or more expressive.

## **Version 0.2**

### **What I changed**

**1\. `→` now means only physical direction or transfer.**

In v0.1 it meant direction, movement, destination, transfer, and sometimes a generic semantic relationship. That was too overloaded.

v0.2 restricts it:

PERSON → PLACE

\= person moves toward place.

OBJECT → PERSON

\= object moves/transfers toward person.

It may not mean "is located at", "uses", "belongs to", or arbitrary relation.

This sacrifices expressive power for predictability.

**2\. I removed `⇄ EXCHANGE` from Core.**

`WATER ⇄ MONEY` was elegant formally but not blind-readable as "for sale".

Instead I add one concrete commercial primitive:

**SALE**

Visual metaphor: an object displayed together with a coin/price-token in a fixed badge.

Its semantic meaning is deliberately broad but bounded:

> obtainable in exchange for money.

Thus:

SALE  
 WATER

\= water for sale.

This is one extra lexical convention, but removes a much more abstract grammatical convention.

**3\. MONEY no longer means payment, price, cost and money simultaneously.**

MONEY means only:

> monetary quantity/value.

SALE supplies the commercial relationship.

Thus:

SALE(WATER)

\= water for sale

while:

SALE(WATER)  
   MONEY: ◇

\= unknown monetary value associated with the sale.

That is the protocol's price query.

**4\. `◇ UNKNOWN` is removed from the human Core.**

This was probably v0.1's least guessable grammatical invention.

Instead v0.2 introduces one explicit **QUESTION frame marker**.

Visual metaphor: an open-ended speech bubble containing the questioned content, with a visibly empty terminal slot.

Conceptually:

QUESTION \[ TOILET \+ LOCATION \]

rather than:

TOILET → ◇

The exact graphic still needs testing, but the important change is semantic: the user does not need to infer "diamond \= unbound variable".

For machines, an unknown variable can still exist in the underlying semantic representation.

Human and machine rendering no longer have to be literally identical.

**5\. Questions are whole frames, not missing graph nodes.**

This removes several scope problems.

QUESTION \[ WATER \+ AVAILABLE \]

\= Is water available?

QUESTION \[ TOILET \+ WHERE \]

\= Where is the toilet?

This requires one additional primitive, **WHERE**, discussed below.

**6\. I add `WHERE` as the only interrogative primitive besides QUESTION.**

This is a deliberate vocabulary increase.

Why?

Because location questions are extremely frequent and the previous:

TOILET → ◇

was fundamentally opaque.

Visual metaphor for WHERE:

a location marker surrounded by outward search rays / multiple possible directions.

Meaning:

> location unknown/requested.

So:

QUESTION \[ TOILET \+ WHERE \]

has a much narrower possible interpretation than v0.1.

I am **not** adding WHO, WHAT, WHEN, WHY, HOW, etc.

**7\. PRESENT becomes AVAILABLE and its meaning is narrowed.**

`PRESENT` was overloaded between exists, here, possessed, accepted and possible.

v0.2 uses:

**AVAILABLE**

Meaning:

> obtainable/usable in the current practical context.

Visual metaphor: object presented on an open palm/surface.

Therefore:

QUESTION \[ AVAILABLE(WATER) \]

≈ Do you have water? / Is water available?

This deliberately does **not** mean philosophical existence or ownership.

**8\. REQUEST gets its own grammatical frame rather than a hand-arrow rebus.**

The hand was overloaded between request and stop.

v0.2 keeps a cupped/open-upward hand as the **REQUEST frame marker**.

Anything visually resting above the receiving hand is requested:

    WATER  
    \_\_\_\_\_\_  
      hand

\= water, please / I request water.

No arrow.

This solves:

HAND → X

being confused with movement.

STOP uses a front-facing raised palm and is visually/semantically separate.

**9\. TAXI remains outside Core.**

The critique showed that:

VEHICLE ⇄ MONEY

does not reliably mean taxi.

But adding TAXI to Universal Core merely because it appears in our examples would violate the design discipline.

Therefore v0.2 says:

REQUEST \[ paid vehicle \]

is **not an acceptable exact encoding of TAXI**.

Taxi must come from a Transport extension or external reference.

This is a deliberate admission of a Core limitation.

**10\. Modifier placement is no longer free spatial grammar.**

v0.1 used "small symbol above entity".

At small sizes this produces uncertain scope.

v0.2 uses a fixed **modifier slot attached to the upper-left edge of an entity tile**.

Conceptually:

┌───────┐  
│❄      │  
│ WATER │  
└───────┘

COLD modifies WATER because it occupies the defined modifier position, not merely because it happens to be nearby.

Only one level of modifier nesting is permitted in Core messages.

**11\. Every semantic unit gets a tile boundary when composition is nontrivial.**

Simple isolated icons need no box.

But composed concepts do:

┌─────────┐  
│ COLD    │  
│ WATER   │  
└─────────┘

This solves scope and machine-segmentation problems.

It also makes:

/  
\[PERSON → PLACE\]

unambiguously negate the entire frame.

**12\. Negation becomes redundant by design in safety contexts.**

The critique found the worst failure mode in the system:

> if the slash disappears, DON'T ENTER can become GO/ENTER.

So safety prohibition must use **two signals**:

1. slash across the prohibited frame;  
2. STOP marker attached to the frame.

Conceptually:

STOP  
╱ \[ PERSON → PLACE \]

If either feature is damaged, the remaining feature still discourages the action.

For ordinary non-safety negation, slash alone remains allowed.

**13\. Number notation is simplified rather than extended.**

Native Core quantities are now explicitly limited to **1–5**.

They use literal countable dots:

•  
••  
•••  
••••  
•••••

No special five-cluster grammar.

Six is:

••••• •

if space permits, but it is no longer considered a preferred native quantity representation.

For values above 5, v0.2 recommends the external numeric layer.

This sounds less capable, but fixes the false elegance of the v0.1 five-group notation.

There is no invented visual arithmetic.

**14\. Quantity marks get a dedicated compartment.**

Instead of dots floating beside an icon:

┌───┬────────┐  
│•••│ BOTTLE │  
└───┴────────┘

This distinguishes grammatical quantity marks from dots that happen to occur inside a pictogram.

It also helps machine vision.

**15\. Minimum-size profiles are introduced.**

v0.1 implicitly assumed the same composition could simply be shrunk.

v0.2 defines two rendering levels:

**Icon profile, 24–32 px:** one primitive or one very simple primitive \+ operator.

**Message profile:** composed frames; minimum practical size determined by number of components.

Nested sentences are simply forbidden at 24–32 px.

This is not new vocabulary. It is a rendering constraint.

**16\. Lexical icons may not contain grammatical marks.**

A lexical pictogram cannot use:

* arrows;  
* quantity dots in grammatical positions;  
* prohibition slash;  
* frame shapes;  
* question marker;  
* warning triangle.

This addresses machine segmentation and accidental grammar.

## **What I deliberately did not change**

I kept **PERSON, WATER, FOOD, TOILET, PLACE, SHELTER, VEHICLE, BOTTLE, CARD and MONEY** as concrete entity primitives.

I kept **COLD and HOT** because their physical metaphors remain relatively strong.

I kept **EYE/SEE**, although it has not yet justified much usage. Removing it merely because we have not used it would be premature.

I kept **STOP** because it provides useful safety redundancy.

I kept **AFFIRM/YES**, despite its dependence on discourse context. Short dialogue genuinely requires affirmation, and a check-like confirmation mark is more practical than attempting to derive YES compositionally.

I kept **WARNING** as a frame-level operator.

I kept **NEGATION** as a slash because it is simple and visually strong. The change is redundancy in safety-critical uses, not replacement.

I kept **containment**:

WATER inside BOTTLE

because physical containment maps unusually well to semantic containment.

I kept sequential reading from left to right for multiple frames. This is culturally imperfect, but replacing it without evidence would solve no concrete problem identified in the critique.

I did **not** add:

ME, YOU, HERE, THERE, HELP, HOSPITAL, TRAIN, BUS, TAXI, TICKET, ENTRANCE, EXIT, LEFT, RIGHT, TIME, DAY, NIGHT, GOOD, BAD, BIG, SMALL.

The critique showed gaps involving some of these concepts, but not that each deserves Universal Core status.

I also did not attempt to solve dates, decimal prices, bus numbers or telephone numbers inside Core. They remain external numeric/reference data.

And I kept **external references**, while explicitly acknowledging that they are an escape mechanism rather than visual-language vocabulary.

## **Final grammar rules**

There are now **nine**.

**Rule 1: Entity.**  
A primitive may stand alone.

WATER

means the concept water.

**Rule 2: Modification.**  
A property occupies the fixed modifier slot of an entity.

\[COLD: WATER\]

\= cold water.

Only one Core modifier layer is allowed.

**Rule 3: Containment.**  
Literal graphical containment means physical/content containment.

BOTTLE\[WATER\]

\= bottle containing water.

It may not be used for arbitrary semantic grouping.

**Rule 4: Quantity.**  
A dedicated quantity compartment contains literal countable marks.

\[••• | BOTTLE\[WATER\]\]

\= three bottles of water.

Core-native counting is optimized for 1–5. Larger values use the numeric extension when literal counting becomes inefficient.

**Rule 5: Direction/transfer.**

A → B

means A physically moves or is transferred toward B.

It has no generic relational meaning.

**Rule 6: Frame operators.**

Operators explicitly enclose their scope:

QUESTION\[X\]  
REQUEST\[X\]  
WARNING\[X\]  
NEGATE\[X\]  
SALE\[X\]

No operator scope is inferred merely from proximity.

**Rule 7: Location question.**

QUESTION\[X \+ WHERE\]

asks for X's location.

`WHERE` has no other grammatical function.

**Rule 8: Dialogue sequence.**

Independent frames are read in displayed sequence:

AFFIRM | \[COLD:WATER\] | SALE\[WATER\]

No syntactic dependency between frames may be assumed except immediate affirmation/negation of the preceding question.

**Rule 9: Safety redundancy.**

A safety-critical prohibition must encode prohibition twice:

WARNING\[PLACE\]  
STOP \+ NEGATE\[PERSON → PLACE\]

Loss of one mark should not turn prohibition into instruction.

That last rule captures the largest philosophical change from v0.1. **v0.2 no longer treats minimum symbol count as the primary objective. It minimizes the amount of invisible grammar the recipient must reconstruct.** The Core remains tiny, but where there is a conflict between formal compositional elegance and blind interpretability, v0.2 deliberately chooses the latter.

One important correction to my prior assumptions: current Pictiq is substantially more developed than just the handbook-level system. It now has an 87-icon canonical registry, a 46-item Universal Core pack, separate vocabulary classifications, entity symbols, numeric notation, Embodied/Standalone profiles, and a versioned machine message format. [GitHub](https://github.com/markoblogo?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

[Pictiq repository](https://github.com/markoblogo/pictiq?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The comparison is interesting because there is quite a lot of convergence, but often at different architectural levels.

### **1\. Design goals**

**Classification: near-identical solution.**

Independent v0.2: a deliberately limited protocol for short practical communication across language barriers and human-machine interaction, explicitly not a replacement for natural language.

Pictiq: describes itself almost exactly at that architectural level: a "minimal visual protocol for short messages across language barriers", aimed at pointing, quick signs, stickers and short composable phrases, not long-form writing. It explicitly prioritizes communicative meaning over reproducing source-language wording. [GitHub](https://github.com/markoblogo?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The independent principle I called **semantic graceful degradation** also has a close Pictiq counterpart in `MEANING-BEFORE-WORDING`, `PRIORITY-FIRST COMMUNICATION`, and `FUNCTIONAL SUFFICIENCY`.

This is genuine independent convergence on the same product definition.

### **2\. Core vocabulary strategy**

**Classification: same underlying principle, different implementation.**

Both systems independently arrived at:

> keep Core small; compose first; add vocabulary only under demonstrated pressure.

My proposed admission rule was roughly frequency → composition → guessability → compression benefit.

Pictiq has formalized this considerably further as **NEED-BEFORE-VOCABULARY**:

existing primitive → composition → context → polysemy → intentional omission → only then consider a new symbol.

It also explicitly rejects adding concepts merely because they are common or theoretically useful.

The engineering difference is scale. My v0.2 tried to hold Universal Core to **23 semantic primitives**. Current Pictiq's `universal-core` contains **46 IDs**, while its ordinary canonical registry is larger still. Pictiq therefore spends more vocabulary to reduce compositional pressure; my experiment deliberately pushed harder toward minimality.

Neither figure alone tells us which architecture communicates more efficiently.

### **3\. Visual metaphors**

**Classification: same underlying principle, different implementation.**

Both prioritize iconic/physical metaphors over arbitrary glyphs: water should visually evoke water, cold should evoke cold, etc.

But Pictiq imposes a much stronger visual identity:

> every lexical word is a monochrome rounded-square tile.

My v0.2 instead allowed object silhouettes, frame operators and structural marks to have different visual forms.

Engineering trade-off:

Pictiq gains strong token segmentation, visual consistency, physical-card usability and machine detection.

My approach potentially gives operators more immediate perceptual distinction from nouns, but creates a less uniform recognition problem.

This is especially relevant at 24–32 px.

### **4\. Composition**

**Classification: same underlying principle, substantially different implementation.**

Both systems strongly prefer:

> primitive \+ primitive → useful meaning

over dedicated symbols for every compound.

But my initial design went much further into **2D semantic composition**:

property slots, containment, frames, arrows, nested semantic units.

Pictiq deliberately does not. Its current machine format explicitly says frames are **flat ordered token sequences** and rejects syntax trees, nested groups and arbitrary 2D semantic positioning for now.

Pictiq's principal construction is:

`BASE + QUALIFIER`

read left-to-right.

Engineering trade-off is unusually clear:

**My design:** richer structural semantics with fewer lexical items, at the cost of more grammar and harder parsing.

**Pictiq:** weaker compositional syntax but much simpler human scanning, rendering, serialization and computer vision.

This directly corresponds to the failure I found in my own v0.1 stress test.

### **5\. Grammar**

**Classification: same underlying principle, different implementation.**

Both independently rejected natural-language grammar.

Neither tries to encode English subject-verb-object structure.

My v0.2 proposed semantic operations such as:

`MODIFIER(X)`  
`REQUEST[X]`  
`QUESTION[X]`  
`A → B`

Pictiq instead uses primarily:

`tile + tile + operator`

with postfix operators and contextual adjacency.

Pictiq is actually **more deliberately underspecified**. Its grammar says bare adjacency is contextual semantic association, not a fixed subject/object, possession or adjective/noun relationship.

That is a major architectural difference.

My system tries to make semantic relationships structurally explicit. Pictiq more often lets practical context resolve them.

### **6\. Questions**

**Classification: superficial similarity at v0.2; major implementation difference.**

Both eventually have an explicit QUESTION mechanism.

But the path is different.

My v0.1 initially invented:

`◇ = unknown semantic variable`

and:

`TOILET → ◇`

for "Where is the toilet?"

After stress-testing, I rejected that as too abstract and v0.2 introduced QUESTION plus WHERE.

Pictiq does something considerably simpler:

`need_toilet + punct_question`

Its question tile deliberately covers several pragmatic readings:

> where / how / is there / can I

So Pictiq uses **contextual interrogative polysemy** where my v0.2 started lexicalizing the distinction with WHERE.

That is an excellent experimental comparison: does `TOILET + QUESTION` outperform `QUESTION[TOILET + WHERE]` in blind comprehension despite carrying less explicit semantics?

### **7\. Yes/no**

**Classification: near-identical solution.**

Both independently concluded that YES deserves a dedicated dialogue primitive rather than being compositionally derived.

Both also allow NO to function independently.

Pictiq has:

`logic_yes`  
`logic_no`

My design had:

`AFFIRM`  
`NEGATION`

The semantics are very close, although Pictiq's `logic_no` deliberately spans no/not/forbidden/closed/not-ok depending on context.

### **8\. Negation**

**Classification: same underlying principle, different implementation.**

Both independently chose a visually explicit negative operator based around the familiar prohibition/slash concept.

But syntax differs fundamentally.

Mine:

`NEGATE[X]`

with the slash applying visually to the entire frame.

Pictiq:

`X + logic_no`

with postfix scope over the preceding semantic unit.

Pictiq additionally allows a physical shortcut of manually drawing a diagonal slash over printed products, while canonical examples use the token.

My v0.2 added **mandatory redundancy for safety-critical prohibition**, such as STOP \+ NEGATE\[action\]. I did not find an equivalent general Pictiq rule.

That is one of the more interesting ideas unique to my independent design.

### **9\. Quantity and numbers**

**Classification: same underlying principle, different implementation.**

This is strong convergence.

Both systems independently concluded:

1. small practical quantities deserve lightweight native representation;  
2. exact/large numbers should be a separate notation layer;  
3. ordinary vocabulary should not contain one symbol per arbitrary number.

My proposal settled on literal countable marks optimized for 1–5 and external numeric representation beyond that.

Pictiq uses:

`qty_1`, `qty_2`, `qty_5`, `qty_plus`, `qty_minus`

with additive composition:

`qty_1 + qty_2 = 3`

`qty_5 + qty_5 = 10`.

Pictiq additionally has a separate **Numeric Notation** architecture for exact written numbers, already partially implemented.

The trade-off is nice:

My literal dots maximize blind countability.

Pictiq's dedicated quantity tiles make token boundaries and machine parsing cleaner and allow MORE/LESS directly.

### **10\. Direction and relations**

**Classification: same underlying principle, different implementation.**

My v0.2 retained a directed `A → B`, but after the critique restricted it strictly to physical movement/transfer.

Pictiq instead has a relational family:

`rel_greater`  
`rel_lesser`  
`rel_up`  
`rel_down`  
`rel_here`

and explicitly warns that these are not generic movement operators. Navigation readings are contextual.

So both systems independently noticed the same engineering danger:

> arrows become semantically overloaded extremely easily.

But they resolved it differently.

Mine narrowed an arrow to physical directed change.

Pictiq separates orientation/reference relations and leaves actual transport/movement largely lexical (`move_feet`, transport concepts, etc.).

### **11\. Context and polysemy**

**Classification: near-identical underlying principle, Pictiq much more developed.**

This may be the strongest conceptual convergence.

I argued that exact lexical reconstruction is unnecessary when practical context supplies the missing information.

Pictiq makes this normative. It explicitly permits action-relevant polysemy, such as transport → ride, coins/card → payment operation, mouth → mouth/eat/drink/oral intake, while forbidding extension into misleading unrelated meanings.

But Pictiq discovered an architectural distinction I did not:

**Embodied Communication vs Standalone Communication.**

That is significant.

Pictiq formally treats pointing, gaze, body, visible objects and environment as part of the communication system in live use. Standalone artifacts must externalize whatever those channels previously supplied.

My design talked about physical context, but I did not elevate **embodiment itself into a first-class protocol layer**.

### **12\. Proper names/entities**

**Classification: same underlying principle, different implementation.**

My proposal:

> Core communicates meaning; Reference identifies things.

I suggested `REF[X]`, potentially containing text, image, QR, ID, etc.

Pictiq independently separates ordinary vocabulary from **Entity Symbols**. Proper names are not Core lexical words; scoped entity symbols have namespaces, provenance, authority and stable reuse rules.

Pictiq's solution is considerably stricter. It tries to keep named entities visual and governed.

Mine is more general because `REF[X]` can point outside the visual language entirely.

Trade-off:

Pictiq preserves visual-system integrity and provenance.

REF offers almost unlimited interoperability but can cease to be human-readable visual language at all.

### **13\. Handling missing meaning**

**Classification: same underlying principle, Pictiq has a more explicit taxonomy.**

I proposed:

> preserve operational consequence before linguistic fidelity.

And suggested explicit machine metadata such as:

`precision: approximate`.

Pictiq now distinguishes things such as intentional omission, lossy representation and actual vocabulary/grammar gaps, and its machine pipeline is supposed to fail loudly rather than silently substitute similar concepts.

This is stronger than my design operationally.

But my proposed explicit **approximation metadata** is conceptually somewhat different: the encoded message itself could declare reduced semantic precision.

That could still be useful to compare against Pictiq's current gap/loss framework.

### **14\. Vocabulary-growth governance**

**Classification: near-identical solution.**

This convergence is striking.

My rule was essentially:

> compose first, lexicalize only when evidence says composition fails.

Pictiq's accepted rule is:

> existing primitive → composition → context → polysemy → intentional omission → new symbol only afterward.

Pictiq additionally has `DEFERRED_UNTIL_NEEDED`, formal classification, context packs, Standalone Core, semantic migration/deprecation rules and CI validation.

So the independent idea exists in Pictiq, but Pictiq has turned it into an actual governance process.

### **15\. Human readability**

**Classification: same goal, different optimization.**

My v0.2 increasingly tried to make grammatical relations explicit.

Pictiq optimizes much more aggressively for **short visual sequences and contextual inference**.

Its recommendation is only 1–3 tiles, hard maximum 5\.

It also has **Catalog Grid Mode**, where icons are not sentences at all but individually pointable concepts.

That is something my independent design did not discover.

This is important because it changes the problem from:

> "Can the recipient decode this sentence?"

to sometimes:

> "Can the recipient point to the concept they need?"

For zero-training practical communication, those are fundamentally different interaction models.

### **16\. Machine readability**

**Classification: same underlying principle, radically different data model.**

Both reject the idea that a digital machine should have to infer semantics again from pixels when semantic data is already available.

I proposed:

visual rendering \+ underlying semantic graph.

Pictiq has actually implemented the specification for:

natural language → semantic decomposition → concept selection → composition → canonical Pictiq Message → validation → renderer.

But its canonical message is intentionally a **flat token sequence**, not my semantic graph.

For example, Pictiq messages carry explicit token types (`icon`, `entity`, `number`), schema version, Pictiq version, profiles and contexts.

Trade-off:

My graph could encode relations more precisely.

Pictiq's flat representation is dramatically easier to validate, serialize, render deterministically and keep aligned with what the human actually sees.

That is perhaps the deepest architectural difference between the two systems.

Several of those are not merely superficial similarities. They are independently reached architectural decisions.

## **Pictiq ideas my design did not discover**

The most important are:

**Embodied vs Standalone Communication.** Pictiq explicitly treats the person's body, gaze, gesture, pointing and physical environment as part of the available communication bandwidth.

**Zero-intent.** A bare tile is intentionally allowed to mean roughly "this / need this / where is this" with interaction resolving the exact speech act. I instead tried harder to encode REQUEST and QUESTION explicitly.

**Catalog Grid Mode.** Communication does not always need sentence formation. A pointable vocabulary surface is a separate canonical interaction mode.

**Standalone Core.** Pictiq recognizes that concepts unnecessary during face-to-face communication may become necessary when the communicator disappears.

**Explicit action-relevant polysemy policy.** I used polysemy, but Pictiq has a clearer rule for when it is legitimate.

**Flat-message discipline.** Pictiq deliberately refuses semantic trees and arbitrary 2D grammar until empirical pressure demonstrates the need.

**Parametric concepts.** The current machine format can attach parameters such as color to an icon without turning every color into another lexical item.

**Vocabulary role classification.** Canonical, Core, Standalone Core, Contextual, Specialized and Mechanism are distinct categories rather than one vocabulary list.

**Fail-loudly machine semantics.** Unknown IDs, ambiguous entities, unsupported parameters and invalid numbers must not be silently approximated by the deterministic layer.

These are meaningful architectural discoveries beyond the system I produced from first principles.

## **Ideas from my independent design that Pictiq does not currently use**

The clearest ones are:

**Explicit semantic graph.** My machine representation separates entity, relation, property, quantity, target and scope structurally. Pictiq deliberately remains flat.

**2D grammatical slots.** My modifier position, containment and scoped operator frames make spatial structure grammatical. Pictiq explicitly avoids arbitrary 2D semantic positioning at present.

**Physical containment as grammatical containment.**

`BOTTLE[WATER]`

has structural meaning in my system. Pictiq would normally use adjacency/context instead.

**Typed physical-transfer arrow.** My restricted `A → B` explicitly represents movement/transfer between arguments. Pictiq has relational/orientation operators but no equivalent universal binary transfer relation.

**Explicit WHERE.** My v0.2 decided that location questions deserved a distinct primitive. Pictiq intentionally lets the generic question operator plus context carry this.

**AVAILABLE as a bounded state.** Pictiq largely obtains availability/acceptance pragmatically from a tile plus question/yes/no/context.

**Safety redundancy rule.** My v0.2 requires critical prohibitions to survive loss of either STOP or NEGATION. Pictiq has compositional and fixed negations, but I found no comparable general double-encoding requirement.

**Explicit approximation metadata.** My proposed machine layer could say that a message is intentionally approximate. Pictiq has a richer translation-quality taxonomy, but not quite this same semantic-message property.

**Resolution profiles.** My v0.2 explicitly prohibited complex compositions at 24–32 px and separated icon-size from message-size grammar. Pictiq has perceptual QA and scale concerns, but not the same grammatical size profile in the specifications I examined.

> preserve operational consequence before linguistic fidelity.

Pictiq formalizes closely related ideas as `MEANING-BEFORE-WORDING`, `PRIORITY-FIRST COMMUNICATION`, and `FUNCTIONAL SUFFICIENCY`.

Both independently arrive at the idea that successful communication is the criterion, not reconstruction of the source sentence.

**3\. Composition before vocabulary expansion.**  
Classification: **near-identical solution.**

My rule was:

> compose first, lexicalize only when empirical evidence says composition fails.

Pictiq's governance sequence is:

> existing primitive → composition → context → polysemy → intentional omission → new symbol.

The Pictiq version is more developed, but the architectural idea is essentially the same.

**4\. Small Core plus domain extensions.**  
Classification: **same underlying principle, different implementation.**

I proposed:

Universal Core

↓

Domain vocabulary

↓

External Reference

Pictiq has a richer hierarchy:

Canonical Registry, Core, Standalone Core, Context Packs, profiles and Entity Registry.

Same scaling problem, independently solved through layered vocabulary rather than one giant dictionary.

**5\. Proper names kept outside ordinary vocabulary.**  
Classification: **same underlying principle, different implementation.**

I proposed `REFERENCE[X]`.

Pictiq has scoped Entity Symbols with namespaces, provenance and authority.

Both prevent Paris, Anton, a hotel, a company, etc. from polluting ordinary semantic vocabulary.

**6\. Explicit yes/no mechanisms.**  
Classification: **near-identical solution.**

Both concluded that YES and NO are frequent enough pragmatic operations to deserve direct representation rather than elaborate composition.

**7\. Explicit question mechanism.**  
Classification: **same underlying principle, different implementation.**

My v0.2 abandoned the abstract unknown-variable diamond and introduced `QUESTION[frame]`.

Pictiq uses terminal `punct_question`.

Both eventually conclude that a visible question operator is preferable to deriving interrogation indirectly.

**8\. Negation as a reusable operator.**  
Classification: **same underlying principle, different implementation.**

I used a slash over a scoped frame.

Pictiq canonically uses:

X \+ logic\_no

and allows a physical slash as a shortcut.

So the semantic architecture converges while scope/rendering differs.

**9\. Small practical quantities separate from general numeric notation.**  
Classification: **near-identical principle, different notation.**

I independently concluded that a visual protocol should handle things such as 1–5 directly but should not reinvent arithmetic merely to represent `18,742`.

Pictiq similarly separates pragmatic `qty_*` tokens from its Numeric Notation layer.

This is a particularly strong convergence.

**10\. Context-dependent polysemy is acceptable.**  
Classification: **near-identical principle.**

I allowed semantic neighbourhoods and argued that exact lexical recognition is not always necessary.

Pictiq explicitly permits action-relevant polysemy, such as transport → ride or coins/card → payment operation.

**11\. Graceful semantic degradation.**  
Classification: **near-identical principle.**

My example was reducing:

> This bridge may collapse. Do not enter.

to something closer to:

> DANGER. DO NOT GO THERE.

Pictiq's intentional omission / functional sufficiency architecture addresses essentially the same problem more formally.

**12\. Machine representation should not simply be computer vision over pictures.**  
Classification: **same underlying principle, different implementation.**

I proposed a semantic payload accompanying visual rendering.

Pictiq already has a canonical machine-readable Message Format with typed icon/entity/number tokens, validation and deterministic rendering.

That convergence is structurally important.

## **Pictiq ideas my independent design did not discover**

Here the differences become more interesting.

### **1\. Zero-intent**

This is probably the most important thing I did not independently derive.

Pictiq says a bare tile is already a valid message:

WATER

can pragmatically mean:

> water / this water / I need water / where is water?

depending on situation.

My architecture kept trying to explicitly encode REQUEST, AVAILABLE, QUESTION, etc.

That made it semantically cleaner but grammatically heavier.

The engineering trade-off is substantial:

**Pictiq:** ambiguity is intentionally delegated to interaction and context.

**Independent protocol:** more intent is encoded explicitly in the artifact.

Neither choice is merely cosmetic. They optimize different communication conditions.

### **2\. Embodied vs Standalone communication**

I discussed context extensively, but I did not make **presence of the communicator** a first-class architectural distinction.

Pictiq does.

That is a genuinely useful idea I missed.

For example, in person:

WATER

plus pointing/body language may be sufficient.

On an unattended airport sign, those channels disappear.

Pictiq therefore asks whether meaning belongs in the icon at all before creating vocabulary.

My design mostly treated the visual message as the communication channel itself.

### **3\. Catalog Grid Mode vs Phrase Line Mode**

I did not discover this distinction.

Pictiq explicitly has:

* grid \= unordered concepts intended for pointing;  
* line \= ordered compositional message.

That's a strong solution to a practical UI problem: the same vocabulary can function both as a communication board and as a sentence-like protocol without pretending the grid itself has syntax.

### **4\. Bare adjacency without fixed relation**

My independent design tried hard to make relationships formally typed.

Pictiq permits:

A \+ B

to mean contextual semantic association without claiming it always means adjective+noun, possession, subject-object, etc.

That is a major architectural divergence.

Pictiq accepts ambiguity early and constrains message length.

I tried to remove ambiguity through explicit semantic relations.

### **5\. Strict phrase-length budget**

Pictiq recommends 1–3 tiles and caps a phrase at five.

I recognized that long composition becomes rebus-like, but did not turn that observation into such a hard grammar constraint.

That's significant because it prevents compositionality from becoming an excuse for arbitrary visual sentences.

### **6\. Vocabulary classification beyond Core/context**

My model was simpler:

Core

Extension

Reference

Pictiq distinguishes mechanisms, Core, Standalone Core, contextual, specialized vocabulary, profiles, canonical registry and entities.

This provides substantially more governance metadata without necessarily adding more visual complexity to a user's message.

### **7\. Explicit GAP as a legitimate result**

I argued that the system should admit that it cannot express something exactly.

Pictiq goes further operationally by distinguishing states such as intentional omission, lossy representation and actual vocabulary/protocol gap.

That distinction is useful for development because:

> "we intentionally don't say this"

is very different from:

> "the protocol currently cannot say this."

My design recognized the general problem but did not formalize the taxonomy as far.

### **8\. Parameterized meaning**

Pictiq's machine architecture can treat something such as color as a parameter rather than requiring RED, BLUE, GREEN, etc. as neighboring lexical symbols.

I did not independently arrive at parametric tiles.

This is potentially important for keeping vocabulary small.

### **9\. Contextual fixed negations**

I insisted strongly on compositional negation.

Pictiq allows dedicated fixed-negative signs where signage or context justifies them, while keeping them out of general Core logic.

That's a pragmatic exception my independent architecture did not consider.

## **Ideas from my independent design that Pictiq does not currently use**

Based on the current repository specification, several ideas remain distinct.

### **1\. Semantic graph as the canonical machine representation**

My original machine model was closer to:

entity: bottle

content: water

quantity: 3

property: cold

with explicit typed relationships.

Pictiq Message Format v0.1 deliberately does **not** do this. Its frames are flat ordered token sequences and explicitly defer syntax trees, nested groups and semantic graphs.

This is one of the biggest structural differences.

The trade-off:

**flat sequence** is easier to author, validate, render and keep aligned with the human representation.

**semantic graph** provides clearer machine scope and relations, but risks creating a machine language substantially more sophisticated than the human protocol.

### **2\. Explicit physical-transfer relation `A → B`**

Pictiq has relational/orientation operators, but does not currently define my restricted:

A → B

as a universal physical movement/transfer relation.

I introduced it specifically after discovering that generic arrows were dangerously overloaded.

Whether this relation is actually understandable enough to justify itself remains empirical.

### **3\. Explicit containment as grammar**

My proposal used literal containment:

BOTTLE\[WATER\]

for:

> water contained in bottle.

Pictiq currently uses flat adjacency and explicitly defers arbitrary 2D semantic grouping.

This is a clean experimental difference.

Containment could be highly intuitive because physical topology mirrors semantic topology, but it also creates rendering, small-size and machine-segmentation complexity.

### **4\. Explicit modifier slots**

v0.2 proposed fixed visual locations inside a tile/frame for modifiers rather than relying on sequence alone.

Pictiq is fundamentally postfix/linear:

X \+ modifier

Again, this gives us a good experimental comparison between **spatial grammar** and **linear grammar**.

### **5\. Safety redundancy as a formal rule**

I proposed that safety-critical prohibition should intentionally encode the prohibition twice, for example STOP plus negation, so damage to one mark cannot invert the message.

I don't see an equivalent general redundancy rule in the current Pictiq grammar.

This is less about vocabulary than error-resistant protocol engineering.

### **6\. Explicit resolution profiles**

I proposed separate constraints for 24–32 px icons versus larger composed messages.

Pictiq does have perceptual QA and multiple physical layouts, so the underlying concern exists, but my proposal made **composition complexity conditional on rendering size** as part of protocol behaviour.

That exact rule is not currently part of Pictiq grammar.

### **7\. Lexical glyphs forbidden from containing grammatical marks**

I proposed reserving certain visual primitives exclusively for grammar so machine vision can distinguish lexical artwork from syntax.

Pictiq's mandatory tile boundaries already solve part of this problem in a different way, but the stronger "reserved visual grammar alphabet" idea is not the same thing.

## **The most useful experimental differences**

If the goal is actually to learn something from this independent exercise rather than merge ideas immediately, I would test the differences before changing Pictiq.

The highest-value experiments are these:

| Experimental variable | Pictiq | Independent v0.2 | What to measure |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Request | bare tile / context | explicit REQUEST frame | blind comprehension vs speed |
| Question | postfix question tile | question frame | question recognition and scope |
| Location question | broad question/context | explicit WHERE | exact recovery vs vocabulary cost |
| Relation | contextual adjacency | explicit `A → B` | ambiguity vs learning burden |
| Containment | adjacency | spatial nesting | `bottle of water`, `person in car`, etc. |
| Modifier | postfix tile | fixed modifier slot | scope accuracy |
| Negation | `X + NO` | scoped slash/frame | blind recognition |
| Safety prohibition | ordinary negation | redundant STOP \+ negation | error under partial occlusion |
| Machine format | flat tokens | semantic graph | parser reliability and authoring complexity |
| Core philosophy | context-heavy | explicit-intent-heavy | task success with/without live communicator |

And I would run the same messages in at least **two conditions**:

**Embodied:** sender and receiver are physically together and can point.

**Standalone:** receiver sees only the visual artifact.

That is particularly important because Pictiq explicitly predicts that the optimal representation may differ between those conditions.

The most scientifically interesting comparison, to me, is not Pictiq versus my v0.2 as two whole systems. That introduces too many variables. It is **controlled A/B replacement of individual mechanisms while keeping the same pictograms and scenarios**.

For example, take exactly the same WATER and BOTTLE artwork and compare:

Pictiq:

WATER \+ qty\_1 \+ qty\_2

against a spatial representation equivalent to:

\[••• | BOTTLE\[WATER\]\]

Then measure blind interpretation, task success, exact semantic recovery, response time, five-minute learned performance and retention a day later.

That would tell us something much more useful than an architectural argument: **whether linear conventional grammar or spatially iconic grammar actually transfers more meaning per unit of required learning.**

The broader result of this comparison is interesting in itself. Despite independent starting points, there is substantial convergence on the high-level architecture: small vocabulary, composition, context, semantic compression, controlled growth, separate treatment of numbers and proper entities, and machine-readable semantics. The largest divergence is lower down: **Pictiq systematically exploits context and controlled ambiguity, while my independent design kept trying to make relations explicit.** That difference is concrete enough to test rather than resolve philosophically.
