Here is the explanation of the visual communication system designed for the dialogue:

1. Distinct Recurring Symbols, Meanings, and Metaphors

Person Profile Icon

Meaning: Speaker / Person / Turn-taking actor.

Visual Metaphor: A classic silhouette profile of a head, universally recognized in signage to represent a human speaker or participant in a dialogue.

Open Hand / Reaching Hand

Meaning: Request / Question / "Do you have...?"

Visual Metaphor: An open palm facing outward or upward, a cross-cultural gesture for asking, requesting, or offering.

Water Waves

Meaning: Water / Liquid.

Visual Metaphor: Three parallel wavy lines, standard pictographic convention for water or liquids.

Thumbs Up

Meaning: Affirmation / "Yes" / Agreement.

Visual Metaphor: The standard global physical gesture indicating approval, confirmation, or an affirmative answer.

Snowflake

Meaning: Cold / Refrigerated / Chilled.

Visual Metaphor: A standard, easily readable geometric snowflake icon used globally on appliances and packaging to signify cold temperature.

Storefront / Canopy

Meaning: Shop / Market / Available for sale.

Visual Metaphor: A classic shop awning silhouette, instantly signifying commercial retail or goods available for purchase.

Water Bottle

Meaning: Bottle / Unit of liquid container.

Visual Metaphor: A simple outline of a standard ribbed plastic water bottle.

Credit Card / Terminal

Meaning: Card payment / Electronic payment.

Visual Metaphor: A payment terminal or card silhouette, directly referencing standard POS (point of sale) point-of-purchase iconography.

Coin / Round Token

Meaning: Unit of local currency / Money.

Visual Metaphor: A simple round coin with an inner rim, universally signaling physical currency without relying on specific regional currency symbols (e.g., $, €, ¥).

2. How Specific Concepts Were Represented

Water: Represented by the wavy lines tile (and combined with the bottle shape for bottled water).

Question: Represented by the open-hand tile preceding the item (asking/requesting gesture) combined with sequence context.

Yes: Represented by the thumbs-up tile.

Cold: Represented by the snowflake tile.

For sale: Represented by the storefront icon tile.

Three bottles: Represented by repeating the bottle symbol across individual tiles (1 bottle tile + 1 bottle tile + 1 bottle tile, or grouped across tiles).

Card payment: Represented by the credit card / payment terminal tile.

Six units of currency: Represented by six identical coin tiles placed in sequence.

3. Meanings Requiring Composition or Context

Asking a question ("Do you have...?"): Created by combining [Speaker Profile] + [Open Hand] + [Water Waves]. The open hand in front of the object converts the statement "water" into an inquiry or request for water.

"For sale": Derived by placing the [Storefront] symbol alongside [Water] and [Cold], placing the item in a commercial context rather than requiring a dedicated "sale" verb.

"Card payment?": Expressed by placing [Bottle tiles] (the items requested) followed by [Credit Card], using position to imply "paying for these with card."

4. Omitted Linguistic Details

Politeness markers ("please"): Omitted, as practical transaction signs prioritize direct semantic units over pragmatic fluff.

Grammatical links & articles ("Do you...", "of local...", "for"): Omitted completely in favor of direct concept-mapping.

Literal distinction between "water" and "bottled water" in Line 1: Line 1 presents generic water/liquid, which is refined to packaged bottle units in Line 3.

5. Representation of Quantities (3 and 6)

To completely avoid Arabic numerals ($3$, $6$) or written numbers while maintaining a strict single-concept-per-tile design:

Quantity 3 (Three bottles): Expressed through tally repetition. Three consecutive bottle tiles are shown sequentially ($1 + 1 + 1$).

Quantity 6 (Six units of currency): Expressed similarly via visual 1:1 correspondence. Six consecutive coin tiles ($1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1$) are displayed across the row to represent the exact amount of six currency units.
