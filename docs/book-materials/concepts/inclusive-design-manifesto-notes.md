# Inclusive-design manifesto notes

> Status: future publication material / critical notes  
> Inputs: Russian and English manifesto drafts archived under `../research/inputs/perplexity-2026-09/`  
> Rule: the manifestos are source material, not Pictiq specification.

## Strongest formulations to preserve

- “Pictiq encodes useful facts and needs rather than assuming who the typical person is.”
- “A symbol system meant for broad use should not pre-decide who is the default and who is the exception.”
- “Grammar and composition can reduce the need for endless identity-category icons.”
- “Inclusive design can be an emergent result of vocabulary minimization, composition, contextual extension, and scoped identity.”
- “The protocol should encode the distinction required for the decision, not every distinction available in the source language.”

## Ideas already supported by current architecture

- Neutral `person_generic` rather than default man/woman icons.
- Structural gender neutrality in Odyssey Stage 1 and the protocol.
- Entity Symbols for named people and narrative entities.
- Context packs and specialized vocabulary instead of Core expansion.
- Numeric notation as a separate mechanism for exact numbers.
- Functional/practical meaning as the main acceptance criterion.

## Claims needing external evidence

- Public signage debates around gender-neutral bathrooms and family pictograms.
- Unicode emoji skin-tone history and whether skin-tone modifiers created new neutrality debates.
- Accessible Icon Project interpretation and community reception.
- Claims about ARASAAC reach, PECS/AAC market structure, and AAC device regulation.
- Claims that competitors lack grammar, or that Pictiq is unique in a broad historical category.

## Formulations that are too absolute

- “Pictiq never represents gender, disability, age, nationality, or marital status.”
- “Age is always better as a number than as a category.”
- “Disability should always be represented only as a functional need.”
- “Loved person replaces husband/wife/partner in every use case.”
- “Pictiq is the only visual language without categories of normality.”
- “All other pictographic systems are flat dictionaries.”

These may work as provocative draft language, but they should not enter `PROTOCOL.md` without qualification.

## Book / article material

Useful future angles:

- an essay on “the data a visual language chooses not to encode”;
- a chapter on how minimal vocabularies can reduce social assumptions;
- a case study connecting road-sign compression, Odyssey gender neutrality, Entity Symbols, and AAC caution;
- a Medium/Substack post contrasting “inclusive by category proliferation” with “inclusive by lower default assumptions.”

## Should not enter PROTOCOL.md now

- Manifesto rhetoric.
- Competitive uniqueness claims.
- Unverified external history claims.
- Absolute bans on identity categories.
- Commercial claims about AAC, public services, or market size.

## Recommended treatment

Keep both language versions. They may evolve into separate publication drafts. Any future public manifesto should cite verified sources and preserve the architecture's domain-dependent precision rule.
