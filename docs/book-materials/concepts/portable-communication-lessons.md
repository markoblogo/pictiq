# Portable Communication Lessons

> **Status:** Concept note
> **Added:** 2026-09-09
> **Source:** [Portable Visual Communication Precedents](../research/portable-visual-communication-precedents.md)

## Photography vs abstraction

Point It is the strongest comparison for photography as communication medium. Photographs can solve concrete object recognition better than symbolic abstraction because they preserve material, scale, packaging, and familiar visual detail. The tradeoff is that photographs age, carry cultural assumptions, and do not provide grammar by themselves.

For Pictiq, this suggests a testable question rather than a settled rule: when does a Pictiq tile outperform a photograph, and when does a photograph outperform a tile?

## Flat vocabulary vs composition

Point It, ICOON, Kwikpoint travel guides, and This, Please appear to use visual vocabulary plus pointing and context. The research pass did not find published formal visual grammars for these products. PECS is different: it has formal interaction phases, but its pictures are not a pictographic language in the same sense as Pictiq.

Pictiq's composition, negation, quantity, relational operators, profiles, context packs, entity symbols, and machine-readable IDs are real architectural differences. They should be described neutrally, not as automatic superiority.

## Context specialization

Kwikpoint and ICOON show that scenario-specific vocabulary can matter more than vocabulary size. Kwikpoint's military, medical, emergency, and training products point toward vertical specialization. ICOON for refugees shows how a tourist dictionary had to adapt to medical, shelter, registration, war/flight, clothing, maps, and civic concepts.

For Pictiq, this supports context packs as research objects: pack quality should be judged by task fit and practitioner review, not just icon count.

## Physical object as interface

Paper, cards, booklets, laminated sheets, and communication books can remain useful beside smartphones because they require no battery, work in noisy or stressful environments, can be shared between people, and make pointing visible to both parties.

Future Pictiq physical tests should measure pocket size, one-hand use, reading distance, category navigation, density per spread, repeated high-frequency concepts, and whether a phrase line or catalog grid is easier in the moment.

## Protocol vs vocabulary

PECS is the strongest adjacent case for separating pictures from interaction rules. Its core innovation is the taught exchange sequence: who initiates, what is exchanged, how the partner responds, and how the learner progresses through phases.

Pictiq can learn from the broader idea that pictures become communicative through protocol and roles. It should not position itself as a clinical AAC replacement.

## Adoption is not intrinsic quality

The precedents suggest several adoption mechanisms: low price, portability, repeated editions, institutional procurement, humanitarian partnerships, clinical/educational training, professional validation, and conventional publishing distribution.

Distribution is not the same as communicative effectiveness. Pictiq should keep those claims separate.

## Open questions for Pictiq

- Which Pictiq concepts need standalone symbols, and which work better through body, context, or phrase composition?
- Which physical format should be tested first: wallet card, booklet, laminated foldout, poster, app companion, or printable sheet?
- Which context packs require practitioner input before public claims?
- When should Pictiq use abstract icons, photographic supplements, text labels, or a hybrid?
- How should Pictiq measure success: recognition, task completion, repair after misunderstanding, retention, or repeated-use fluency?

Related stress-test domains are tracked in the [scenario bank](../experiments/scenario-bank.md).
