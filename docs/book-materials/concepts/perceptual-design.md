# Perceptual design

## Core observation

A structurally valid icon is not necessarily a perceptually successful icon.

## How it was discovered

Small-size, grid, and layout review exposed failures that source validation could not see, including difficult morphology in person, fire, and other silhouettes.

## Evidence

[Icon spec](../../../spec/ICON_SPEC.md#perceptual-design-principles), [Visual QA Protocol](../../../spec/ICON_SPEC.md#visual-qa-protocol), [Batch A QA note](../experiments/batch-a-visual-qa.md).

## Current status

Normative review workflow. Reference-driven assets still require the same QA; established morphology is preserved when recognition depends on it.

## Unresolved questions

Controlled human recognition and physical-size studies remain future work.

## Potential book angle

Why visual minimalism is an operational constraint rather than a style preference.

## Comparative research note

The [comparative visual-language review](../research/from-pictographs-to-protocols.md) records the useful tension that iconicity can help initial interpretation, familiarity stabilizes interpretation, context narrows possibilities, distinctiveness reduces confusion, and training creates convention. “Universal” should therefore mean demonstrated performance in specified contexts and populations, not an aesthetic property of an icon.
