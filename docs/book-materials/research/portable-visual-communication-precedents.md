# Portable Visual Communication Precedents

> **Status:** Research source / case-study archive  
> **Added:** 2026-09-09  
> **Relationship to Pictiq:** Comparative precedent research, not protocol specification.  
> **Terminology:** These systems are treated as precedents, analogues, and adjacent systems, not as direct ancestors of Pictiq and not merely as competitors.

## Why these cases matter

Point It, Kwikpoint, ICOON, This, Please, and PECS all solve communication problems with visual representations, but they do so through different architectures. Some are portable pointing dictionaries. Some are commercial or institutional reference products. PECS is different again: its core value is a formal interaction protocol around pictures, not a standalone picture dictionary.

They are useful to Pictiq because they show how portable communication depends on medium, scenario, physical format, user training, vocabulary selection, distribution, and institutional trust. They also show where Pictiq should be cautious: adoption, photographic specificity, professional validation, and repeated real-world use are not automatic consequences of a clean symbolic system.

The tourist dictionary products are best described as visual vocabulary plus pointing plus situational context. That does not mean they have no communicative structure in real use: users can sequence pointing, gesture, facial expression, speech fragments, and environmental context. The research pass did not find evidence of a published formal visual grammar comparable to Pictiq for Point It, Kwikpoint, ICOON, or This, Please.

## Point It

### What it is

Point It is a small photographic travel picture dictionary by Dieter Graf. The current Graf Editions page presents it as the original picture dictionary and lists the 22nd edition, 9 x 13 cm size, 72 pages, ISBN 978-3-980-8802-7-5, and price of EUR 8.90 in Germany in 2025 ([Graf Editions](https://www.graf-editions.de/en/pointit)).

### Origin and creator

Dieter Graf is consistently credited as author/creator across the publisher page, book records, and retail listings. A 2023 Welt article reports that the idea originated during travel in India in 1976 and that Graf later chose photos because they preserve realism and scale better than drawings ([Welt](https://www.welt.de/244020869)). This is useful but should be cited as reporting/interview material rather than independent archival proof.

### Design decision

Photography is the defining design choice. It solves object specificity: a photographed object can show scale, material, packaging, and real-world visual cues. It also reduces the drawing-convention burden for concrete objects. Its limitation is the opposite: photographs can age, carry cultural assumptions, and handle abstract relations poorly without surrounding language or gesture.

### Communication model

The model is pointing at a photo in a physical book. There is no verified formal grammar, negation system, or compositional syntax. A user can combine pointing with gestures and situational context, but that is an interaction practice rather than a published grammar.

### Physical/product format

The strongest physical facts are 9 x 13 cm and 72 pages from Graf Editions. Internet Archive metadata for a 2009 copy records 72 pages, 10 x 13 cm, and 50 g ([Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/pointittraveller0000graf)). Google Books records a 2007 illustrated 14th reprint with 62 pages ([Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Point_it.html?id=iowbPwAACAAJ)), which shows edition variation.

### Evolution and adoption

Graf Editions states "Edition since 1992" and lists a 22nd edition. Retail and book pages often say the book contains about 1,300 or 1,400 items; the official page and Stanfords/Amazon listings support this range ([Stanfords](https://www.stanfords.co.uk/point-it-traveller-s-language-kit-9783980880275)). AbeBooks/publisher copy reports more than 2 million copies sold, while the supplied "2.5 million" wording was not confirmed by a stronger source in this pass ([AbeBooks](https://www.abebooks.com/Point-Travellers-Language-Kit-Dieter-Graf/32454011159/bd)).

### Interesting users/use cases

Publisher and retail copy report use by tourists, UN peacekeeping forces, Olympic athletes, and speech therapists. This should be treated as publisher-copy evidence unless a separate institutional source is found ([ISSP Shop](https://shop.issp.lv/en/products/point-it)).

### What made it durable

Its durability appears to come from low price, pocket size, no power requirement, fast pointing, and photographic specificity. The claim that it remains useful despite smartphone translation is supported by recent journalism and the current publisher page, but it is not a quantified effectiveness claim.

### Limitations relative to its own design goal

It is strongest for concrete objects and situations. It is weaker for grammar, abstract ideas, negotiation, negation, quantity, temporal relations, and cases where a photograph is too culturally specific or visually outdated.

### Relevance to Pictiq

Point It is the clearest precedent for physical pointing as a communication interface. It also challenges Pictiq: photographic specificity can outperform abstraction for many concrete travel needs.

### Ideas worth testing

Passport-sized Pictiq guides, repeated high-frequency concepts, physical indexes, small-format pointing tests, and side-by-side tests of photo, pictogram, and Pictiq tile for the same task.

### What Pictiq should not copy

Do not copy photographs, page layouts, trade dress, or the assumption that object coverage alone equals protocol capability.

### Verification notes

Core bibliographic and format claims are VERIFIED or SUPPORTED. The 1976 origin and photo rationale are REPORTED from journalism. The 2.5 million sales claim remains UNVERIFIED; use the more conservative "over 2 million" only with publisher/retail attribution.

### Book-use status

USE WITH ATTRIBUTION. The book itself, creator, format, 1992 publication era, and photographic model are strong. Origin-story and sales claims need attribution.

## Kwikpoint

### What it is

Kwikpoint makes visual-language translators and image-heavy reference publications for travel, military, medical, emergency, security, and training contexts. Its own site describes "minimal text, extensive images" and emphasizes pocket, wallet, full-size, waterproof, tear-resistant, and laminated/folding formats ([Kwikpoint travel](https://www.kwikpoint.com/travel-landing-page-2/)).

### Origin and creator

Kwikpoint identifies Alan Stillman as founder and CEO and says he developed the concept on a 15,000-mile around-the-world bicycle trip after cutting out magazine pictures in Hungary to communicate dinner selections ([Kwikpoint About Us](https://www.kwikpoint.com/about-us/)). The 28-country detail appears in secondary summaries, but the company page found in this pass supports the 15,000-mile trip and Hungary story.

### Design decision

Kwikpoint's design decision is not pure icon language. It is a product method: reduce text, use extensive pictures, match content to mission, and produce durable portable guides. The company describes iterative research, image-driven content development, and usability testing.

### Communication model

The travel products appear to be pointing dictionaries. Military and training products combine visual vocabulary, short text, checklists, tactics/procedures, and context-specific reference. This is closer to a verticalized visual reference system than a general visual grammar.

### Physical/product format

The site reports waterproof, tear-resistant synthetic paper or folding laminate and a range of sizes including pocket-sized products. A 2010 public video-review snippet described an International Translator as 3.75 x 5.5 inches closed and listed a retail price of USD 14.95, but that source is secondary, old, and not used as a durable product fact.

### Evolution and adoption

Kwikpoint reports travel, military, health/safety, law enforcement, corporate, and custom products. Its site claims more than 6 million communication guides supplied to U.S. and foreign military forces, and other pages display "Over 9,000,000 Sold" ([Kwikpoint About Us](https://www.kwikpoint.com/about-us/); [Kwikpoint military](https://www.kwikpoint.com/military-landing-page-2/)). These are first-party commercial claims and should not be repeated as independent adoption proof.

### Interesting users/use cases

Works That Work reports a Marine had used a Kwikpoint travel guide in East Timor and asked for a custom military guide; the article frames the later guides around U.S. troops and locals in Afghanistan and Iraq ([Works That Work](https://worksthatwork.com/1/point-me-where-it-hurts)). Leatherneck repeats the claim that the U.S. Army made Kwikpoint standard issue for soldiers stationed in Iraq and Afghanistan, but this pass did not locate a stronger government procurement source ([Leatherneck](https://www.leatherneck.com/forums/showthread.php?26415-Cards-help-deployed-troops-bridge-language-gap=&p=155207)).

### What made it durable

Context specialization appears central. Kwikpoint moved from travel into military, emergency, medical, and training products by narrowing vocabulary around high-stakes scenarios and by using procurement/custom production channels.

### Limitations relative to its own design goal

The public web evidence is mostly company material and design journalism. Product-specific symbol counts, procurement scope, and current public availability are unevenly documented.

### Relevance to Pictiq

Kwikpoint is important for context packs. It suggests that the same visual mechanism becomes more useful when vocabulary is selected for a specific mission, surface, and user group.

### Ideas worth testing

Pictiq vertical packs, laminated cards, scenario-specific guides, practitioner-reviewed vocabularies, and task tests where the same core icon appears in different context packs.

### What Pictiq should not copy

Do not copy Kwikpoint layouts, illustrations, military wording, product taxonomy, or procurement claims. Do not overclaim institutional adoption without primary procurement evidence.

### Verification notes

Founder, broad verticals, and first-party company claims are SUPPORTED. The East Timor/military pivot is REPORTED through design journalism. "Standard issue for all servicemembers" did not survive; the stronger retained wording is that military-oriented Kwikpoint products were reportedly used/supplied in Iraq/Afghanistan contexts, with first-party and secondary support but no independently verified universal-issue claim.

### Book-use status

USE WITH ATTRIBUTION. The vertical-specialization story is valuable but must be sourced carefully.

## ICOON

### What it is

ICOON is a picture dictionary family by Berlin designer Gosia Warrink / AMBERPRESS. The current ICOON site describes a global picture dictionary for nonverbal communication and lists product variants including ICOON eco, ICOON for refugees, ICOON+, and an app ([ICOON](https://www.icoon-book.com/)).

### Origin and creator

Publishing Perspectives reports that Warrink designed ICOON in 2007 and printed it through AMBERPRESS ([Publishing Perspectives](https://publishingperspectives.com/2016/01/icoon-a-book-of-symbols-for-refugees/)). Book-market records also place ICOON Global Picture Dictionary with Amberpress in 2007 ([AbeBooks UK](https://www.abebooks.co.uk/)).

### Design decision

ICOON combines symbols, pictograms, and photos. The current site says ICOON eco contains over 2,000 symbols, pictograms, and photos, while the app page lists over 1,100 symbols across categories. The refugee edition uses 1,200 symbols/pictograms/photos and adds questions, answers, and statements in multiple languages.

### Communication model

The basic model is pointing at category-organized images. The refugee edition moves closer to an aided first-contact tool because it includes multilingual questions, answers, statements, maps, and a humanitarian interaction setting. No formal grammar comparable to Pictiq was found.

### Physical/product format

The refugee edition is documented as 52 pages, 12 categories, maps, laminated softcover, saddle stitching, and ISBN 978-3-9809655-5-2 in the Warrink/Pfau chapter reproduced by De Gruyter/Transcript sources ([De Gruyter/Brill](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783839434536-024/html)).

### Evolution and adoption

The strongest source is the chapter by Gosia Warrink and Monika Pfau in *Gefluechtete und Kulturelle Bildung*. It states AMBERPRESS had published ICOON since 2007; in 2015 requests came from the German Red Cross and volunteer initiatives; AMBERPRESS donated over 2,000 books from July to September 2015; then developed a DIN A4 first-aid sheet with 300 symbols and ICOON for refugees; and the first 30,000 books were distributed in December 2015, followed by 20,000 more in February 2016 ([De Gruyter/Brill](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783839434536-024/html)). SPRYLAB reports 65,000 total circulation, EUR 23,023 crowdfunding revenue, and 1,200 symbols/photos for the app/book project ([SPRYLAB](https://sprylab.com/en/icoon-for-refugees-app/)).

### Interesting users/use cases

The refugee adaptation was driven by border, shelter, registration, medical, and everyday-help contexts. Sources mention German Red Cross personnel, refugee helpers, doctors, humanitarian workers, and volunteers. Vocabulary examples include polio, trauma, scratches, head coverings including hijabs or burkas, peace, democracy, and law.

### What made it durable

ICOON's strength is category structure plus domain adaptation. The humanitarian pivot shows that a tourist vocabulary does not simply transfer to refugee aid; content must be revised with practitioners and users.

### Limitations relative to its own design goal

The refugee sources are rich but often project-authored. The system is not a general formal visual language, and its humanitarian version was intentionally constrained to first communication.

### Relevance to Pictiq

ICOON is the best precedent for context packs driven by domain practitioners. It shows why a general core needs special vocabularies for health, clothing, documents, safety, identity, rights, and local procedures.

### Ideas worth testing

Practitioner-informed Pictiq packs, first-contact sheets, category-specific repeated concepts, refugee/travel/medical scenario tests, and separation of free humanitarian aids from commercial editions.

### What Pictiq should not copy

Do not copy ICOON symbols, photos, category pages, app interface, wording, brand, or humanitarian positioning. Do not assume a humanitarian context without domain partners.

### Verification notes

Creator, 2007 origin, refugee pivot, Red Cross/volunteer involvement, 1,200-symbol refugee edition, 30,000/20,000 print runs, 2,000 donated books, and several vocabulary examples are VERIFIED/SUPPORTED by strong project-authored and publisher/academic sources. Product-wide counts vary by edition and must always carry date/edition.

### Book-use status

READY for the refugee-pivot facts when cited to Warrink/Pfau or SPRYLAB. USE WITH ATTRIBUTION for broader adoption and current-product claims.

## This, Please

### What it is

*This, Please: The Tourist Picture Dictionary* is a conventional publisher-led tourist picture dictionary from Michael O'Mara Books. Its value as a precedent is that it shows the pointing-picture dictionary as a mature publishing format, not only as a founder-led communication-system story.

### Origin and creator

The available records credit Michael O'Mara Books / Michael O'Mara Books Limited rather than a named founder-designer. No credible founder narrative was found in this pass.

### Design decision

The book uses pictures for tourist pointing scenarios. Amazon copy says the user flips to a page and says "This, please"; chapters include dining, transport, leisure, and activities ([Amazon.com](https://www.amazon.com/)). This is commercial-positioning copy, not an architectural specification.

### Communication model

The model appears to be publisher-selected images plus tourist context. No evidence of a formal visual grammar, machine-readable layer, or maintained protocol was found.

### Physical/product format

Book records agree on ISBN 9781843175735 and a 64-page paperback. StoryGraph lists publisher Michael O'Mara and edition publication date 1 June 2011 ([StoryGraph](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/)); Kinokuniya lists 64 pages ([Kinokuniya](https://united-states.kinokuniya.com/)); Waterstones lists dimensions 140 x 165 x 4 mm and weight 113 g ([Waterstones](https://www.waterstones.com/)).

### Evolution and adoption

No substantial edition history was verified. Current retail traces exist on Amazon, Waterstones, Kinokuniya, Medimops, eBay, and other bookseller pages, but availability varies by marketplace.

### Interesting users/use cases

The positioning is general tourist use: food, transport, leisure, activities, and practical requests. This is useful because it reflects category packaging rather than a specialized protocol.

### What made it durable

Traditional book distribution, low cognitive burden, and the familiar tourist phrasebook genre likely explain its appearance. This is an inference from product metadata and category positioning, not a verified adoption history.

### Limitations relative to its own design goal

The product has weak public documentation. It is useful as a category signal but not as a richly evidenced communication-system case.

### Relevance to Pictiq

It shows that "point at a picture while traveling" is a recognizable retail premise. It also shows that Pictiq should separate protocol claims from book-format claims.

### Ideas worth testing

Publisher-style compact editions, tourist scenario grouping, and market tests around whether users understand a Pictiq physical object as a phrasebook, card, symbol system, or app companion.

### What Pictiq should not copy

Do not invent a founder narrative. Do not copy page layout, illustrations, title formula, or publisher positioning.

### Verification notes

Publication facts are SUPPORTED by bookseller and bibliographic sources. Picture count, edition history, and product performance are UNVERIFIED.

### Book-use status

NEEDS MORE VERIFICATION for narrative use. READY only for narrow bibliographic statements with citations.

## PECS / A Picture's Worth

### What it is

PECS, the Picture Exchange Communication System, is an augmentative and alternative communication approach developed by Andy Bondy, PhD, and Lori Frost, MS, CCC-SLP. It is included here as an adjacent interaction-protocol case, not as a tourist product analogue and not as a clinical substitute category for Pictiq.

### Origin and creator

PECS.com states that PECS was developed in the United States in 1985 by Bondy and Frost and first implemented with preschool students diagnosed with autism at the Delaware Autism Program ([PECS.com](https://pecs.com/)). Pyramid pages identify Bondy and Frost as PECS developers and Pyramid founders.

### Design decision

PECS is built around exchange and initiation. The official six phases begin with exchanging single pictures for desired items or activities, then expand distance/persistence, picture discrimination, sentence structure, attributes/language expansion, responsive requesting, and commenting ([PECS six phases](https://pecs.com/)).

### Communication model

The pictures are not the whole system. The protocol specifies teaching sequence, prompting, exchange behavior, communication partner response, and later sentence strips and attributes. The picture representation can vary; the core innovation is the interaction routine that teaches functional communication.

### Physical/product format

PECS uses picture cards, communication books, training materials, and professional workshops. It is not a fixed pocket dictionary.

### Evolution and adoption

Pyramid describes itself as the worldwide source for PECS and the Pyramid Approach, with global offices and trainings ([Pyramid USA](https://pecsusa.com/)). This supports institutional/professional network adoption, but this pass did not attempt a clinical efficacy review.

### Interesting users/use cases

The intended audience is parents and professionals working with children and adults with autism and related disabilities who do not speak or have limited communication abilities. That audience distinction must be preserved.

### What made it durable

Durability appears tied to training, manualized phases, professional networks, evidence/practice framing, and a clear learner-partner interaction model.

### Limitations relative to its own design goal

PECS is a clinical/educational AAC method. It should not be generalized casually to travel signage, commercial phrasebooks, or public pictographic language design.

### Relevance to Pictiq

PECS provides the sharpest distinction between icon vocabulary and interaction protocol. Pictiq can learn conceptually from the fact that pictures become communicative through rules and roles, while staying outside clinical AAC replacement claims.

### Ideas worth testing

Explicit partner-role tests, repair routines, initiation/response sequences, and studies of how a Pictiq tile changes meaning when used in a pointing exchange versus a standalone printed line.

### What Pictiq should not copy

Do not copy PECS materials, clinical claims, phase wording, training program structure, or imply Pictiq is a clinical AAC replacement.

### Verification notes

Founders, qualifications, 1985 origin, Delaware Autism Program context, six-phase structure, and relationship between *A Picture's Worth* and PECS are VERIFIED/SUPPORTED by official PECS/Pyramid and book sources.

### Book-use status

READY for the vocabulary-vs-protocol distinction and narrow historical facts with citation. DO NOT USE Pictiq as a clinical comparison without expert review.

## Book-relevant answers

### Point It: what changes when the medium is photography?

Photography solves concrete-object recognition better than many abstract pictograms because it preserves material, scale, and familiar appearance. It remains weak for abstract grammar, negation, quantity, and culturally variable interpretation. The physical book survives beside smartphones where speed, no battery, no connection, and pointing across a shared page matter.

### Kwikpoint: what does vertical specialization show?

The travel origin matters less than the later adaptation mechanism. Kwikpoint's military, emergency, medical, and training products show that vocabulary becomes more useful when narrowed to a user population, setting, and task.

### ICOON: what happens when tourist vocabulary meets humanitarian use?

The general travel dictionary was not enough. The refugee version needed medical, border, registration, clothing, maps, trauma, war/flight, abstract civic terms, and practitioner input.

### This, Please: what does publisher entry show?

It suggests that pointing picture dictionaries were legible enough as a retail category for a conventional publisher to package one without a large founder-system narrative.

### PECS: what changes when the core innovation is interaction rules?

The pictures become part of a teachable exchange routine. This is conceptually relevant to Pictiq's protocol thinking, but PECS remains a clinical/educational AAC method with its own evidence, ethics, training, and population.

## Adoption mechanisms

| Case | Likely adoption drivers | Evidence caution |
|---|---|---|
| Point It | Portability, low price, photography, repeated editions, travel need, bookshop/museum distribution | Sales and special-user claims mostly require attribution. |
| Kwikpoint | Vertical customization, institutional procurement, laminated formats, military/emergency use cases | Military scope should not be universalized without government sources. |
| ICOON | Practitioner input, humanitarian partnerships, crowdfunding, free distribution, app support, category adaptation | Strongest for 2015-2016 refugee project; current market claims are separate. |
| This, Please | Traditional publisher distribution, tourist phrasebook category | Adoption and picture-count data weak. |
| PECS | Manualized teaching protocol, professional network, training, clinical/educational context | Do not infer Pictiq clinical claims. |

Evidence of distribution is not evidence of communicative effectiveness. Future Pictiq work should measure tasks, misunderstandings, repair, and repeated-use learning separately.

## Physical interface lessons

Portable visual communication works when the object fits the moment. Relevant variables include pocket size, one-hand usability, waterproofing/lamination, category tabs, spread density, repeated critical items, indexability, and whether two people can point at the same surface while negotiating meaning.

For Pictiq, these become test questions: what fits on a wallet card, what requires a booklet, what belongs on a laminated sheet, and when a phrase line is clearer than a catalog grid?

## Copyright and design ethics

Researching a precedent does not authorize copying its creative expression.

Pictiq may legitimately learn from functional category structure, product dimensions, navigation principles, density tradeoffs, scenario specialization, interaction mechanics, and high-level product architecture.

Pictiq should not reproduce copyrighted illustrations, photographs, distinctive page compositions, proprietary symbol sets, brand/trade dress, logos, proprietary wording, or protected taxonomies copied wholesale. Public-information morphology should be independently implemented under current Pictiq rules.

## Ideas worth testing, not yet accepted

- Passport-sized physical guide.
- Scenario or vertical editions.
- High-frequency concepts repeated across sections.
- Practitioner-informed context packs.
- Durable foldable or laminated surfaces.
- Category navigation systems.
- Physical format optimized for pointing.

None of these are accepted roadmap items or protocol changes.
