# Pictiq Games / Human Communication Lab

> Status: DEFERRED / NON-BLOCKING / PLANNED  
> Source event: Games / Human Communication Lab planning pass, 2026-09-13.  
> Boundary: this note does not create a game, printable cards, vocabulary, context packs, grammar, Composer behavior, Renderer behavior, Message Schema behavior, or experiment results.

## Purpose

Pictiq Games / Human Communication Lab is a future research and product track for testing Pictiq through actual play, pointing, gesture, interpretation, repair, and repeated communication.

The track is high-value, deferred, and non-blocking. It should run when time, participants, and a suitable venue are available. Ordinary Pictiq development should not wait for formal human sessions.

The track can serve four purposes at once:

- human communication research;
- game-design experimentation;
- zero-training stress testing;
- possible future commercial product exploration.

No commercially viable game is claimed yet.

## Core philosophy

The games track extends the Zero-Training Entry Principle. Players should ideally not need to know the name Pictiq, study a Pictiq textbook, memorize internal IDs, or learn formal grammar terminology before trying to communicate.

The useful research question is:

> Can people begin communicating by recognizing, selecting, combining, pointing to, moving, and interpreting visual symbols?

Learning during repeated play is not a defect. It may become useful evidence about discoverability, transfer, repair, and emergent convention formation.

## Prototype A — Open Vocabulary Communication Board

Working concept: players can see a large board, table, sheet, or projected surface containing the currently available Pictiq symbols.

A sender receives a phrase, intent, situation, concept, or short message and tries to communicate it by selecting or arranging available symbols. Other players interpret the result.

Possible modes:

- individual sender -> group receivers;
- team -> team;
- timed or untimed;
- free communication;
- scenario-based communication.

Primary research dimensions:

- zero-training concept discovery;
- composition;
- decoding;
- ambiguity;
- repair;
- symbol search;
- emergent conventions.

Rules are intentionally not finalized.

## Prototype B — Embodied Pictiq / Crocodile

Working concept: a Pictiq-enhanced version of charades / Crocodile.

A player receives a target meaning and may communicate using body movement, gesture, pointing, physical positioning, and nearby or wearable Pictiq symbols.

Possible low-cost materials later:

- cards;
- pins;
- Velcro;
- wearable sheet;
- mock T-shirt;
- other cheap temporary mechanisms.

Production T-shirts are not required for testing.

Primary research question:

> What changes when Pictiq symbols are combined with embodied and situational communication?

A future comparison may test gesture only vs Pictiq only vs gesture + Pictiq, but this note does not run or design that comparison.

## Prototype C — Constrained Hand

Working concept: players or teams receive a random subset, or hand, of Pictiq symbol cards.

They receive a target word, phrase, intent, or situation and must communicate it using only available symbols.

Potential mechanics:

- use current hand;
- draw additional symbols at a cost;
- discard or exchange;
- race another team;
- optimize clarity vs number of draws.

Primary research dimensions:

- semantic compression;
- composition under vocabulary scarcity;
- intentional omission;
- substitution;
- repair;
- gap pressure;
- which symbols have high combinatorial value.

Scoring and game economy are not frozen.

## Semantic-slot scoring direction

Future game sessions should avoid relying only on broad subjective ratings such as "I understood 70%."

A useful scoring direction is to decompose the target into required semantic slots.

Example target:

> I want to go to the sea, but I do not have a car.

Possible required slots:

- WANT;
- MOVE / TRAVEL;
- SEA;
- CAR;
- NEGATION.

Other slots should be added only when the chosen target genuinely requires them.

A receiver gives a natural interpretation. The analysis can then compare recovered required slots against total required slots. This remains a future scoring direction, not a finalized rubric.

## Game success vs research success

Game success and research success are different.

A research instrument can produce useful observations while being boring. A commercial game must be enjoyable enough that people voluntarily play, replay, compete, develop strategies, and talk about it.

Future Pictiq Games work should track both dimensions. The research track remains useful even if no commercial game results.

## Emergent conventions

Repeated play may reveal emergent conventions. Groups may begin to:

- reuse the same compositions;
- assign stable meanings;
- develop shortcuts;
- create ordering conventions;
- combine gesture and symbols consistently;
- teach conventions implicitly to new players.

These observations should not automatically become normative Pictiq. Record them as evidence, pressure, and possible later design input.

## Physical first

Preferred future prototyping sequence:

PAPER / PRINTABLE PROTOTYPE -> LOCAL PLAY -> RULE ITERATION -> REPEAT PLAY -> DIGITAL VERSION ONLY IF WARRANTED.

Physical cards, boards, and sheets are cheap to modify and naturally support pointing, gesture, proximity, and embodied communication. A digital game should not come first merely because Pictiq already has web tooling.

## Digital later

Potential later digital versions may support:

- remote play;
- automated target generation;
- timing;
- scoring;
- corpus collection;
- cross-language teams;
- replay and analysis.

These are future product options, not current implementation tasks.

## Real-world testing opportunity

A future testing path may exist through tabletop-game contacts, a nearby game club, people accustomed to trying new games, or potential access to game-publishing expertise if a prototype proves fun.

Do not treat this as confirmed commercial interest.

## Commercial possibility

If one mechanic becomes genuinely enjoyable, Pictiq Games may become a standalone commercial product.

Possible paths:

- physical card game;
- board / communication game;
- party game;
- educational / family game;
- digital adaptation.

Commercialization is optional and evidence-dependent.

## Relation to Stress Test 04B

Stress Test 04B remains READY / NON-BLOCKING / RUN WHEN OPPORTUNITY EXISTS.

04B tests Composer authoring. Games test communication behavior. They are different.

Game-based sessions may eventually provide more naturalistic, higher-volume, and more engaging human communication observations than a conventional usability session, but they do not replace 04B.

## Relation to zero-training

Games are especially relevant to zero-training entry because they can expose:

- first-turn comprehension;
- first-turn production;
- time to useful communication;
- repair behavior;
- improvement over repeated rounds;
- transfer to unseen targets;
- emergent conventions.

Do not claim those outcomes before testing.

## Relation to semantic compression

Constrained Hand is especially relevant to semantic compression. It can generate natural examples of people deciding what information to preserve when symbol resources are limited.

Future observations can be classified using existing compression labels:

- PRESERVED_EXPLICITLY;
- PRESERVED_BY_COMPOSITION;
- CONTEXT-SUFFICIENT;
- INTENTIONAL_OMISSION;
- LOSSY;
- GAP.
